# Craft Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-28
**Scope**: Full manuscript (9 chapters, ~21,255 words)
**Review pass**: Third (post-revision)
**Focus**: Subtler craft issues now visible after major pattern thinning; sentence-level prose quality; crutch words; publication readiness

---

## Summary

The two previous prose-tightening passes have improved the manuscript substantially. "The way" is down from 22 to 14 instances -- no longer a distracting pattern. The recon blocks in Ch VI have been varied successfully (data-only, school-specific, cross-register). The manuscript reads cleanly at the chapter level. What emerges now, with the major patterns resolved, are subtler sentence-level issues: one persistent crutch word ("something" at 43 instances), a negation density that occasionally muffles the prose, and a handful of passages where the analytical voice overwrites the emotional moment.

---

## Findings

### M1. "Something" as crutch word: 43 instances across 21,255 words

- **Frequency**: 1 per 494 words (roughly once per half-page)
- **Distribution**: Ch I: 2, Ch II: 1, Ch III: 2, Ch IV: 2, Ch V: 5, Ch VI: 15, Ch VII: 3, Ch VIII: 6, Ch IX: 7
- **Problem**: "Something" is the book's thematic word -- it names the unknowable. Many instances are earned:
  - Ch V, line 77: "something happened that altered his behavioral output" -- load-bearing; the indeterminacy IS the point
  - Ch VIII, line 87: "The host then did something the probe could not interpret" -- correct; the action is unknowable
  - Ch I, line 4: "Something is happening to it" -- the iconic opening beat

  But roughly 15-18 instances are filler rather than thematic:
  - Ch VI, line 25: "something about a trade agreement, something about a school shooting in Missouri, something about a basketball player" -- three "somethings" in one sentence; the first two could become "a trade agreement, a school shooting in Missouri, a basketball player"
  - Ch VI, line 43: "something between recognition and loss" -- could be "a feeling between recognition and loss" or restructured
  - Ch IX, line 17: "the nearest galaxy groups... something about..." -- check exact usage
  - Ch III, line 53: "something that the concept of agency does not cleanly apply to" -- "an entity that the concept of agency does not cleanly apply to"
  - Ch VI, line 143: "The interaction had cost him something" -- "something" is vague here; "a measure of" or simply "cost him" would be sharper

- **Severity**: MEDIUM. At 43 instances in a 21K-word book read in one sitting, "something" becomes the most frequent non-article word pattern. The thematic instances (roughly 25) are essential to the book's voice. The filler instances (~18) dilute the word's power.
- **Suggestion**: Thin to ~28-30 instances by cutting or replacing the filler uses. Preserve every instance where "something" names the unknowable. Cut instances where it is merely vague.

### M2. Negation density: cumulative weight of the negative register

- **Pattern counts**: "does not" (56), "is not" (53), "did not" (32), "was not" (36), "cannot" (28), "could not" (25), "never" (23) = 253 negations across 21,255 words, or 1 per 84 words.
- **Problem**: The book's thesis is about what cannot be known, so negation is structurally appropriate. But at this density -- roughly one negation per 3-4 sentences -- the prose develops a quality of continual refusal. The reader is told what things are NOT more often than what they ARE. This is most noticeable in the outer chapters (I, III, V, IX) where the analytical voice dominates. In Ch VI (Bob's lived experience), the positive register naturally breaks through.
- **Severity**: LOW. This is a feature-not-bug situation for most of the manuscript. The negation register IS the book's voice. Flagging it because a professional editor would note it, not because it needs wholesale revision.
- **Suggestion**: In a final pass, identify 10-15 negations that can be rewritten as positive assertions. Example: "The sphere does not emit probes. It does not transmit messages. It does not announce itself." could become "The sphere grows. The growing is the only signal." (The manuscript already does this in Ch I, line 46, which means the technique is available but not consistently applied.)

### M3. Ch VII (The Counterfactual): analytically rich but emotionally thin in the middle section

- **Location**: Ch VII, lines 23-45 (the birth-year and serotonin perturbations)
- **Problem**: The first perturbation (cloudy weather, lines 4-19) is grounded in specific sensory details: keystroke cadence, guacamole threshold, Gerald Watkins call duration. The deep perturbations (born 1977, serotonin rate) become increasingly abstract. The birth-year section (lines 23-33) lists percentages and scenarios without the sensory grounding that makes the weather perturbation land. The serotonin section (lines 37-45) is philosophical argument rather than embodied counterfactual.
- **Severity**: MEDIUM. Ch VII follows the emotional peak of Ch VI. It needs enough connective tissue to the reader's experience of Bob's Tuesday to maintain engagement. The opening and closing are strong. The middle sags.
- **Suggestion**: Add 2-3 specific sensory details to the birth-year and serotonin sections. For the birth-year Bob: what does his morning look like? What mug does he drink from (or does he not have the mug)? For the serotonin Bob: does the shower thought last 15 seconds instead of 30? Does he notice the scratch on the table? Ground the perturbations in the specific objects the reader now knows from Ch VI.

### L1. Passage where analysis overwrites the emotional moment: Ch VI, lines 237-238

- **Location**: Ch VI, closing recon block, the enumeration of irrecoverable experiences
- **Quoted text**: "What he thought about during the three-minute walk from the parking lot to his office door. The weight of his back against the chair at 3:45 PM, when he looked out the window at a strip of grass and a Wendy's and did not think anything and this was, somehow, necessary."
- **Problem**: This is exquisite writing. But the enumeration that follows it runs for ~200 words of list items, and around the 150-word mark, the accumulation shifts from devastating to exhausting. The list is strongest in its first 5 items (the carpet, the coffee, the shower thought, the lunch-bag pause, Ethan talking about Marcus) and weakest in its last 3 items (the three-minute walk, the shampoo coordinate, the string cheese).
- **Severity**: LOW. The list is deliberately overwhelming. But the last 2-3 items tip from "accumulating weight" into "the writer is not sure when to stop."
- **Suggestion**: Consider cutting 2 items from the tail of the list, ending it at the Maya doorway transaction. The shampoo and string cheese items, while individually lovely, compete with stronger items above them. The strongest ending for the list would be "The feeling at Maya's doorway, the transaction in a currency that does not survive its speakers."

### L2. Minor prose issue: "the man has been dead for twelve hundred and thirty years"

- **Location**: Ch VI, line 249
- **Problem**: Not a craft error per se, but the math implies the narrative present is 1,230 years after Bob's death (2030 + 1,230 = 3,260 CE). The Reorganization started ~2038-2040. "Twelve hundred years" for the sphere's expansion puts the present at ~3,240 CE. Bob died in 2030, so he has been dead for ~1,210 years, not 1,230. The "thirty" is an extra 20 years. This is a borderline craft/consistency issue: the "twelve hundred and thirty" is poetic rounding, but in a passage that also gives Bob's death as "968 days after this Tuesday" (precise), mixing precise and imprecise numbers in the same sentence creates a mild dissonance.
- **Severity**: LOW. The imprecision is within the realm of narrative rounding ("approximately twelve hundred" would cover the range). But the "and thirty" adds false precision.
- **Suggestion**: Either make it precise (calculate the exact figure) or round more clearly ("more than twelve hundred years").

## Strengths

1. **Ch VI's dual-voice mechanism is now mature.** The recon blocks are varied in length, register, and school affiliation. The lunch-bag recon block (the 4.2-second crack in the model) is the chapter's finest technical achievement. The final recon block ("The model is 97.3% accurate and 0.0% complete") is a candidate for the best single passage in the book.
2. **The "the way" construction is now under control.** At 14 instances, the remaining uses are mostly strong (crystal growth, geologist/fossil, Old English recognition). The thinning was well-targeted.
3. **Ch II (The Wavefront) remains the best-crafted chapter for its length.** The acceleration in sentence structure is earned, the "Faster" repetitions create genuine momentum, and the phase-transition passage is the single most effective prose experiment in the manuscript.
4. **The Bob chapter's emotional specificity is extraordinary.** The Cuisinart coffee maker, the bike circles, the corner seat, the three-inch door gap -- these details are not decoration. They are the book's argument, rendered as domestic life. This is the rare science fiction manuscript where the human chapter is the strongest, not the weakest.
5. **The ending ("Light on a river. Four seconds. A Tuesday in March.") is perfect.** The cut from the previous over-explicit thesis was the right call. The images do the work.
