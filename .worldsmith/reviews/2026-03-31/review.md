## MEDIUM Issues

### M1. "conscious, probably" is a mechanical tic (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch III lines 19, 31, 55; Ch IV line 5; Ch VII line 61
- **Quoted text**: "conscious, probably" (5 occurrences)
- **Problem**: The parenthetical hedge works the first time. By the fifth occurrence it reads as a verbal reflex rather than a philosophical position. The reader has internalized the uncertainty by Ch III; repeating the hedge suggests the prose does not trust the reader to remember.
- **Suggestion**: Use "conscious, probably" once or twice (Ch III is the right place to establish it). Thereafter vary: "if conscious they are," "whatever consciousness means at this scale," "experiencing, if experience applies," or simply drop the hedge.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes, by voice-auditor. The voice-auditor confirms this is a Ch III/IV phenomenon that bleeds into Ch VII and should be treated as a voice issue, not just a repetition issue. Agreement on severity (MEDIUM) and recommendation (reduce to 2 uses).

### M2. Ch IV philosophy is indistinguishable from Ch III (source: voice-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch IV lines 49-59 (hard problem passage through measure-theory analogy)
- **Quoted text**: "What the third planet's philosophers called the hard problem of consciousness is, to the Sol-mind, a landmark visible from a great distance..."
- **Problem**: Ch IV's signal-journey passages are voiced distinctly (the Mercury Shell revisions, the Kuiper Halo's slow response). But the philosophical block in the middle reads as a direct continuation of Ch III. If these paragraphs were transplanted into Ch III, no reader would notice.
- **Suggestion**: Embed the philosophy more deeply in the signal narrative. Have the measure-theory insight emerge as a thought the Mercury Shell formulates during its revisions. Or give the philosophical voice a shorter, more staccato rhythm to contrast with Ch III's nested sentences. The content is strong; it needs its own voice.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes, by craft-auditor. The craft-auditor notes that the "third planet's philosophers" / "third planet's mathematicians" phrasing appears in both Ch IV and Ch V in nearly identical constructions (LOW finding CR3), which contributes to the sense that the philosophical voice is undifferentiated across these chapters. Agreement on severity (MEDIUM).

### M3. Ch VII serotonin section loses momentum (source: structure-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch VII, paragraphs beginning "What if Bob's serotonin reuptake rate were 5% higher?"
- **Quoted text**: "The model runs. The behavioral output shifts. The shift is not dramatic: Bob is marginally less prone to the vertiginous shower thoughts, marginally more decisive about lunch..."
- **Problem**: Ch VII has three escalating perturbations: weather, birth year, neurochemistry. The weather perturbation is brilliant (the 3.1% keystroke cadence shift). The birth-year perturbation is moving ("in 8%, he enlists in the military after September 11, 2001"). The serotonin perturbation repeats the weather-perturbation technique (small behavioral shifts, guacamole percentages) without advancing it. The reader has already learned that small changes produce small behavioral differences.
- **Suggestion**: Cut by 40-50%. Keep the opening perturbation and the key insight ("the one parameter changes everything that matters and nothing that is measurable"). Cut the intermediate detail. Deliver the philosophical payload in two paragraphs, not four.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes, by craft-auditor. The craft-auditor confirms the section does not introduce new prose techniques or insights beyond what the weather perturbation already demonstrated. Agreement on severity (MEDIUM) and recommendation (tighten).

## LOW Issues

### L1. Ch II and Ch VIII share identical G5V star specifications (source: consistency-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch II line 7, Ch VIII line 13
- **Quoted text**: Both describe a "G5V, 0.92 solar masses" star system
- **Problem**: These are different systems (94 ly vs 210 ly from Sol), but the identical spectral type creates a subtle impression they might be the same.
- **Suggestion**: Differentiate one slightly (e.g., make the Ch II target a G3V or K0V).

### L2. "behind the wall" approaching refrain density (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch IV line 59, Ch V lines 27/41, Ch VII line 67, Ch IX line 89
- **Problem**: 6 uses of "the wall" / "behind the wall" across 5 chapters. Borderline between intentional refrain and verbal default. The Ch IX use ("The wall does not open for endings") is the freshest.
- **Suggestion**: Prune 1-2 of the most automatic-sounding uses. Ch VII line 67 (about simulation silence) is the weakest candidate.

### L3. "the third planet's" repetition (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch III line 69, Ch IV lines 49/53, Ch V line 27
- **Problem**: "The third planet's philosophers" appears in nearly identical form across three chapters.
- **Suggestion**: Vary after first use: "the pre-conversion species' philosophers," or use proper names (Nagel, Chalmers).

### L4. Ch V forward-references Bob details before Ch VI renders them (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch V, "He is a claims adjuster from Columbus, Ohio..." paragraph
- **Problem**: Details like "corner seat" and "Idaho" are introduced before Ch VI gives them context. This works as anticipation on first reading and as recognition on re-reading.
- **Suggestion**: Judgment call. The catalogue technique creates anticipation. Leave it if intentional.

### L5. Ch VIII probe opening uses human-literary voice (source: voice-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch VIII lines 3-5
- **Quoted text**: "Between the stars, there is time."
- **Problem**: Beautiful but human-sounding. "The star was wrong" (line 11) is a stronger, more alien opening.
- **Suggestion**: Consider swapping the two openings.

### L6. "Something is integrating" could be strengthened (source: voice-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch IX line 55
- **Problem**: The Great Unification beat is structurally the chapter's most important moment. The sentence is understated to the point of thinness.
- **Suggestion**: Add one sentence: "Something is integrating. The structure is observable. What it experiences, if it experiences, is not."

### L7. Ch IX candle metaphor overlaps with Ch VIII simile (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch VIII line 21 ("as a candle is to a star"), Ch IX lines 41/87
- **Problem**: The candle image is most powerful in Ch IX's black-hole context. The Ch VIII use (about intelligence scale) dilutes it slightly.
- **Suggestion**: Consider removing the Ch VIII "candle/star" simile to reserve the image for Ch IX.

### L8. Consciousness hierarchy introduced in a concentrated block (source: structure-auditor)
- **Location**: Ch III lines 17-33
- **Problem**: The full hierarchy (miniaturized entities through Sol-mind) is presented in one dense passage.
- **Suggestion**: Consider introducing miniaturized entities in Ch IV instead, where they serve as the finest-grain perception of Bob's 4.2-second pause.

## Verification Notes

### Epistemological Wall Audit (Full Manuscript)

Every chapter was checked for wall violations -- passages where the text declares what the Sol-mind, the probe, or any post-singularity entity experiences, feels, or thinks, without appropriate hedging. Results:

- **Ch I**: Clean. The sphere "does not emit probes. It does not transmit messages. It does not announce itself." All observational.
- **Ch II**: Clean. "The planet thinks its first thought. What the thought is, what it contains, what it means: these are not questions that have answers at this scale."
- **Ch III**: Clean. "conscious, probably" hedging maintained. "The Sol-mind is a unified agent, probably, at the day-scale."
- **Ch IV**: Clean. "Whether this persistence is a defect of the optimization landscape...is behind the wall."
- **Ch V**: Clean. The five schools are presented as competing interpretations, none authoritative.
- **Ch VI**: Clean. Register B maintains "Class X" / "not recoverable" / "irrecoverable" throughout.
- **Ch VII**: Clean. Simulated Bob's internal states are presented as "something the model classifies as" rather than confirmed experience.
- **Ch VIII**: Clean. "7447-Lyra was not designed to feel obsolescence. It was not designed to feel anything. Whether it felt something nonetheless is a question that applies to the probe in exactly the form it applies to Bob Kessler."
- **Ch IX**: Clean. "Something is integrating" uses observation-from-outside framing. "Whether it is conscious, whether there is something it is like to be a structure whose components are stellar-scale minds, is unknowable for the usual reasons."

**No wall violations found.**
