## Findings

### FINDING C1: Ch II and Ch VIII share identical G5V star specifications (LOW)

**Location**: Ch II line 7, Ch VIII line 13
**Quoted text (Ch II)**: "The star is a G5V, 0.92 solar masses, 0.84 solar luminosities."
**Quoted text (Ch VIII)**: "A G5V, 0.92 solar masses, luminosity $3.2 \times 10^{26}$ watts."

**Problem**: These are different star systems (94 ly vs 210 ly from Sol), but both are described as G5V with 0.92 solar masses. Previous reviews have flagged this. It creates a subtle ambiguity -- a careful reader might wonder if the probe's target is the same system as the wavefront chapter's system.

**Suggestion**: Differentiate one system slightly (e.g., make the Ch II target a G3V at 0.95 solar masses, or a K0V at 0.85 solar masses). This is cosmetic, not a factual error.

**Severity**: LOW. The systems are separated by 116 light-years and are never explicitly identified as the same.

### FINDING C2: "Four seconds" vs "4.2 seconds" -- intentional but worth noting (LOW)

**Location**: Multiple chapters. Ch VI uses "approximately four seconds" and "four seconds." Ch IV introduces the data as "a 4.2-second discrepancy." Ch VII uses both "four seconds" and "4.2-second pause."

**Problem**: Not actually a problem. The "four seconds" is Bob's subjective experience (round, imprecise, human). The "4.2 seconds" is the Sol-mind's measurement (precise, instrumental). The dual usage is thematically appropriate -- it embodies the gap between lived experience and reconstructed data. However, it should be noted that this dual convention is working as intended and requires no correction.

**Severity**: LOW (no action needed; documented for completeness).
