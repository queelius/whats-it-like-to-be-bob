## Strengths

### S1. The dual-voice mechanism in Chapter VI is sustained and effective

The \recon blocks in Chapter VI maintain their register throughout: clinical, data-sourced, confidence-interval-bearing, Class X-marking. The shift into the closing recon block (lines 216-264) -- where Register B breaks its own format and becomes something more than analysis -- is the book's finest voice achievement. The transition from "Subject: Robert Allen Kessler" to "We do not know what it was like to be Bob" is earned by every preceding recon block's restraint.

### S2. Chapter II achieves acceleration through prose structure

The Wavefront chapter's voice is distinct: kinetic, compressing, with one-line paragraphs ("Faster.") that enact the singularity cascade. The sentences genuinely shorten and accelerate through the chapter. The phase transition at line 61 changes the prose's relationship with time, as the outline demanded.

### S3. The probe voice (Ch VIII) successfully occupies the uncanny valley

7447-Lyra's perspective is neither human nor fully alien. The processing-time details (14 milliseconds for a realization, 0.3 seconds for classification) establish a consciousness that measures itself by different units. The phrase "Whether it felt something nonetheless is a question that applies to the probe in exactly the way it applies to Bob Kessler, and with exactly the same answer" (line 15) achieves the structural rhyme the outline calls for without being heavy-handed.

## MEDIUM Issues

### M1. Chapters I and III share too much voice

- **Location**: Chapter I (The Overview) and Chapter III (The System)
- **Finding**: The style guide specifies distinct voices: Ch I is "Naturalist field notes. Present tense. Short declarative sentences." Ch III is "Contemplative, intimate but alien. Long sentences, nested clauses." In practice, both chapters share a similar register: observational, precise, third-person, present-tense, with moderate sentence lengths. Ch I's sentences are slightly shorter on average, but Ch III does not consistently deliver the "long sentences, nested clauses" that the outline promises. Compare:
  - Ch I: "Inside it, stars are going dark. Not dying: their mass persists, their gravitational influence is unchanged, their planets continue to orbit." (Short, declarative)
  - Ch III: "The star called Sol is a G2V, 4.6 billion years old, with 5.4 billion years of main-sequence fuel remaining." (Medium length, still declarative)
  - Ch III should be more like: "A computation initiated in the Inner Corona, near the star, must wait 2 minutes for the Mercury Shell to receive the result." (This is contemplative but still relatively clipped.)
- **Problem**: The voice differentiation between Ch I and Ch III is audible but insufficient. A reader moving from I to III (via the more distinctive II) should feel a clear shift in prose rhythm. Currently the shift is subtle.
- **Suggestion**: Revise Ch III to lean harder into the contemplative voice: longer sentences, more subordinate clauses, more nesting. The passage about Earth (lines 33-39) is closest to the target voice. The shell architecture description (lines 5-12) is still in Ch I's declarative mode.

### M2. Chapter IV voice not sufficiently distinct from Chapter III

- **Location**: Chapter IV (The Gradient) vs. Chapter III (The System)
- **Finding**: The outline specifies Ch IV as "Contemplative. Slow. Two temporal perspectives interleaved." Ch III is "Contemplative, intimate but alien." In practice, both chapters use similar contemplative prose. The key differentiator should be the temporal interleaving -- the sense that we are hearing from different temporal zones within the Sol-mind. Chapter IV achieves this through content (the signal traveling outward, the revisions, the Kuiper Halo's belated response) but not consistently through voice. The prose rhythm in Ch IV is similar to Ch III.
- **Suggestion**: The final paragraph of Ch IV (the Oort Cloud passage, line 67) is the chapter's strongest voice moment -- a single long sentence that enacts the temporal delay. More of the chapter should achieve this quality. Consider varying sentence length to match the temporal zone: short, rapid sentences for the Mercury Shell's fast processing; longer, slower sentences for the Halo's delayed reception.

### M3. Chapter V voice transition could be sharper

- **Location**: Chapter V (The Archive), opening
- **Finding**: The outline specifies Ch V's voice as "Discovery, wonder from an alien perspective. The tone of an archaeologist holding a 3,000-year-old grocery list." The opening -- "The data arrived in layers" -- is clean but doesn't strongly signal a new voice. The chapter's first half (data classes, no-cloning theorem) reads more like Ch III's observational register than the "wonder" the outline calls for. The voice shifts correctly in the second half, when the analysis narrows to Bob.
- **Suggestion**: Inject more of the "archaeologist's wonder" into the early data-class passages. The data itself (120 years of electromagnetic noise, physical artifacts scanned at molecular resolution) deserves more texture. Currently these passages are functional rather than evocative.

### M4. Chapter IX's elegiac voice is achieved but could cool more gradually

- **Location**: Chapter IX (Deep Time)
- **Finding**: The outline specifies: "Elegiac. Slow. Sentences lengthening. Active verbs giving way to passive. Prose cooling toward equilibrium." The chapter begins elegiacally ("Sol has been a white dwarf for three billion years") and maintains the register. However, the voice does not progressively cool across the chapter's 2,458 words as much as the outline envisions. The Great Unification passage (lines 43-59) is energetic and forward-leaning, which appropriately conveys the structural surprise, but the return to cooling after it could be more deliberate. The final paragraphs (lines 119-132) are tonally warm (Bob callback) rather than thermodynamically cool.
- **Problem**: The outline calls for "the sentence that ends the book should be the slowest sentence in it." The final sentence -- "This is the thing the universe built itself to ask about and could not, across $10^{100}$ years of asking, touch." -- is rhythmically moderate, not slow. It is a good sentence but not the slowest.
- **Suggestion**: Consider a final sentence or paragraph that is longer, slower, and more syntactically passive, approaching the equilibrium the chapter describes. The current ending is emotionally effective but tonally at odds with the "cooling toward equilibrium" directive.

## LOW Issues

### L1. The five schools in Chapter V and VII use consistent analytical voices

The Positivist, Modal, Constructionist, Structuralist, and Apophatic voices are appropriately differentiated in their brief appearances. The Structuralist note in Ch VI's Chipotle recon block (line 136) is a particularly fine deployment. No issues.

### L2. Register A and Register B transitions in Ch VI

The transitions between Bob's lived experience and the recon blocks are smooth throughout. The only minor friction is the long recon block at lines 53-57 (the lunch bag pause), which at ~200 words is the longest unbroken recon passage. This length is earned by the content (the 4.2-second crack in the model) but a reader deep in Bob's voice may feel the interruption.

### L3. "The third planet" as alien-voice marker

Chapters III and V use "the third planet" to signal the ASI's perspective. This is effective and consistent. Chapter V's shift to "Robert Allen Kessler" and then "Bob" in the narrowing section (lines 63-95) correctly mirrors the shift from alien distance to intimate proximity.
