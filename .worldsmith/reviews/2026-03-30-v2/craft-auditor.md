# Craft Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-30
**Manuscript**: "What's It Like To Be Bob?" -- Chapters I-IX (~21,000 words)
**Scope**: Full manuscript, style guide, anti-cliche rules

---

## Overall Craft Assessment

The prose quality is consistently high across all nine chapters, with the Bob chapter (VI) reaching the level of excellent literary fiction. The dual-voice structure works. The numbers create genuine sublimity. The style guide's principles -- "hard science, poetic prose," "the sublime through precision," "tenderness toward the small" -- are fulfilled.

The manuscript's principal craft achievement is that it makes 21,000 words feel like a complete and satisfying reading experience despite having no traditional protagonist, no plot, and no dialogue (except Bob's chapter). The zoom structure does the work that plot normally does: it creates momentum, stakes, and payoff through spatial and conceptual movement rather than through narrative events.

---

## Strengths

1. **The dual-voice in Ch VI is the manuscript's crown jewel.** The interleaving of Bob's lived experience with the ASI's reconstruction frames creates a reading experience that is simultaneously warm and clinical, intimate and vast. The `\recon` blocks do not interrupt; they deepen. By the chapter's end, the reader has internalized the gap between data and experience without being lectured about it.

2. **The numbers do the emotional work.** "$5 \times 10^{48}$ operations per second cannot reconstruct" is more moving than any adjective could be. The style guide's principle of "the sublime through precision" is consistently executed. The Landauer limit, the Bekenstein bound, the 97.3%/0.0% split -- these function as emotional instruments, not exposition.

3. **Chapter transitions are earned.** Each chapter arrives at its successor naturally. The zoom-in from galactic to human scale (I through VI) and the zoom-out from human to thermodynamic scale (VI through IX) both feel inevitable rather than arbitrary. The sharpest transition -- from Ch V's archaeological methodology to Ch VI's "feet on the carpet" -- is the book's most powerful structural moment.

4. **The Bob chapter's specificity.** The Aldi coffee, the Kroger loyalty card, the WORLD'S OKAYEST DAD mug, the scratch on the kitchen table, the cereal brands (Cheerios for Maya, Froot Loops for Ethan). These details make Bob real. The reader recognizes their own Tuesdays. Without this specificity, the book's philosophical architecture would float.

5. **The closing sentence is perfect.** "Light on a river. Four seconds. A Tuesday in March." -- This is the slowest sentence in the manuscript, as the style guide prescribed. It earns its simplicity through 21,000 words of precision. It does not explain. It does not summarize. It names three things and stops.

## Findings

### FINDING 1 (MEDIUM): Repetition of "fossil" Metaphor

**Locations**: Ch I line 55, Ch III line 47, Ch IV lines 15, 57, 63, 79
**Pattern**: "fossil," "fossils," "a fossil of a thought," "a fossil from a civilization that no longer exists," "a fossil of a fossil"
**Problem**: The fossil metaphor is used 8+ times across the manuscript to describe how signals become outdated during lightspeed transit. It is effective the first two times. By the fifth occurrence, it has become mechanical. In Ch IV alone, it appears in some variant 4 times. The metaphor is doing real conceptual work (interstellar communication as paleontology), but the single word "fossil" is carrying too much of the load.
**Suggestion**: Vary the expression. "Archaeological," "antiquity," "artifact," "relic" could carry some of the weight. Or use the concept without the word: "a message from an entity that, by the time the signal arrived, had already become someone else."

### FINDING 2 (MEDIUM): "The wall" Metaphor Overuse

**Locations**: Ch IV lines 43-53, Ch V lines 21-28, Ch V line 59, Ch VII lines 74-78, Ch IX lines 33, 53, 71-73, 95
**Pattern**: "the wall," "the wall does not yield," "at the wall," "behind the wall," "another wall"
**Problem**: "The wall" as a metaphor for the hard problem of consciousness is used approximately 15 times across the manuscript. It is the book's central metaphor and does important structural work. But by Ch IX, "another wall" and "behind the wall" have become reflexive rather than resonant. The metaphor's power comes from its first few deployments; by its fifteenth, the reader anticipates it.
**Suggestion**: This is a harder fix than the fossil issue because "the wall" is thematically central. Consider whether some instances could be replaced with the concept expressed differently: "the gap between," "the surface that does not open," "the distinction that no description can cross." Reserve "the wall" for its most load-bearing appearances (Ch IV's introduction, Ch VI's deployment, Ch IX's "the event horizon is the hard problem made physical").

### FINDING 3 (LOW): "Approximately" Frequency

**Locations**: Throughout, especially Ch I and Ch III
**Pattern**: "approximately," appearing 20+ times in the manuscript
**Problem**: The word "approximately" appears frequently, especially in the naturalist-voice chapters (I, III). This is consistent with the scientific register, and each individual usage is correct. But the cumulative effect slightly dulls the precision-as-poetry principle: "approximately" is a hedging word, and hedging words in high density create a tentative tone.
**Suggestion**: Vary with "roughly," "on the order of," or simply present the number with its precision built in (e.g., "$\sim 2 \times 10^{11}$" instead of "approximately $2 \times 10^{11}$"). The tilde notation already does the work of "approximately."

### FINDING 4 (LOW): Ch VII's Counterfactual Section Flattens After the Weather Perturbation

**Location**: Ch VII, second and third sections ("What if Bob had been born ten years earlier?" and "What if Bob's serotonin reuptake rate were 5% higher?")
**Problem**: The first counterfactual (weather) is vivid and grounded: the 73% guacamole finding, the 3.1% keystroke shift. The subsequent counterfactuals (birth year, serotonin) are more abstract and less sensory. The birth-year section, with its enumeration of percentages (47% insurance, 23% sales, 8% military...), reads as a catalogue rather than a narrative exploration. The chapter's energy dips in its middle before recovering with the Constructionist School section.
**Suggestion**: Ground the birth-year and serotonin counterfactuals in specific sensory details the way the weather counterfactual is grounded. Instead of "In 3%, he moves to Chicago," give one sentence of the Chicago Bob: the Brown Line apartment, the deep-dish pizza, the missing Ohio.
**Note**: On re-reading, the birth-year section does include the Chicago detail and the military detail. The issue is not absence of detail but pacing: the percentage-list framing creates a catalogue feel. Consider restructuring to lead with the vivid details and let the percentages emerge from them.

### FINDING 5 (LOW): "The comparison is not meaningful" Pattern

**Locations**: Ch I line 33 ("The comparison is not meaningful. It is offered here and withdrawn."), Ch II line 59 ("The system does not know this comparison.")
**Problem**: The gesture of offering a comparison and then withdrawing it is one of the manuscript's signature moves, and it works well. But the explicit disclaimer "the comparison is not meaningful" appears twice in quick succession (Ch I, Ch II). By Ch II, the reader has already internalized the principle.
**Suggestion**: In Ch II, the withdrawal could be more implicit. The sentence "The system does not know this comparison" already does the work; the explicit disclaimer in Ch I has taught the reader the pattern.

## Scene Mechanics

### Ch VI (Bob): Scene Flow

The Bob chapter's pacing is excellent. The morning routine (6:22-8:03) builds slowly and specifically, establishing the texture of Bob's life. The 8:03 bridge moment is the emotional peak, placed at the chapter's midpoint. The afternoon (work, lunch, Gerald Watkins) provides falling action. The evening (Kroger, puzzle, bedtime) brings resolution. The final `\recon` block breaks format to deliver the chapter's philosophical payload.

The `\recon` blocks increase in frequency and emotional intensity through the chapter:
- Early blocks are purely data-citation (appliance IDs, timestamps)
- Middle blocks begin commenting on the gap (the scratch, the mug)
- The 8:03 block is the pivot: "What the light looked like to Robert Allen Kessler: not recoverable."
- Late blocks become increasingly reflective (the Gerald Watkins simulation comparison, the dream)
- The final block breaks format entirely

This progression is well-crafted. The reader does not notice the blocks escalating until they have already been carried.

### Ch IX (Deep Time): Pacing Across 10^100 Years

The challenge of spanning $10^{100}$ years in ~2,600 words is handled through compression: each era gets a section break and a paragraph or two, with the narrative dwelling longest on the Great Unification (the structural surprise) and the closing (the emotional payoff). The pacing works because the chapter is not trying to be comprehensive; it is trying to be elegiac.
