# Voice Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-30
**Manuscript**: "What's It Like To Be Bob?" -- Chapters I-IX (~21,000 words)
**Scope**: Full manuscript, style guide, voice prototypes, character docs

---

## Overall Voice Assessment

The manuscript executes its per-chapter voice differentiation with remarkable consistency. Each chapter has a distinct register that the reader can feel without being told about it. The naturalist field notes of Ch I, the kinetic acceleration of Ch II, the contemplative nested clauses of Ch III/IV, the discovery-wonder of Ch V, the warm literary fiction of Ch VI, the philosophical vertigo of Ch VII, the disorientation of Ch VIII, and the elegiac cooling of Ch IX -- all are achieved and sustained.

The book's unified sensibility (precision, tenderness toward finitude, comfort with unanswered questions) holds across all nine voices. This is the manuscript's principal stylistic achievement: nine distinct voices that feel like one author.

---

## Per-Chapter Voice Analysis

### Ch I: The Overview (Naturalist)
**Specification**: Observational, present tense, short declarative sentences, numbers woven in.
**Execution**: Excellent. The opening paragraph sets the tone immediately: "The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy, 100,000 light-years in diameter..." Short sentences. Present tense. Numbers as facts of nature, not exposition. The chapter sustains this register for its entire length. The one-sentence paragraph "Something is happening to it." is perfectly placed.
**Voice consistency**: No lapses.

### Ch II: The Wavefront (Kinetic)
**Specification**: Accelerating. Sentences compress as the singularity cascades. One-line paragraphs ("Faster.").
**Execution**: Excellent. The chapter begins at measured pace (the probes arrive, they survey) and accelerates through the bootstrapping sequence. The one-word paragraphs ("Faster.") and the compression of events (months to decades in a single sentence) create genuine kinetic energy. The final lines ("The star is awake. / The system joins the silence. / From outside, it looks like a star that has gone dim. From inside, it is a universe.") slow precisely when they should.
**Voice consistency**: No lapses.

### Ch III: The System (Contemplative)
**Specification**: Long sentences, nested clauses. Ideas unfold over paragraphs.
**Execution**: Strong. The communication-latency sentence (line 17, a single sentence spanning ~150 words with six embedded clauses) is the chapter's voice signature and it works. The nested structure mirrors the nested shells of the Dyson swarm. The contemplative register is sustained throughout.
**Issue**: See below (Finding 1).

### Ch IV: The Gradient (Temporal Dialogue)
**Specification**: Two (or many) temporal perspectives interleaved. Slow, contemplative.
**Execution**: Strong. The chapter successfully creates the feeling of time passing differently in different parts of the same mind. The Mercury Shell's "staccato revisions" voice contrasts with the Kuiper Halo's "broad, slow, patient" voice. The identity-question passage (line 69) shifts to a more philosophical register without breaking the chapter's coherence.
**Issue**: The Kuiper Halo passage (line 57) introduces a new register -- long, recursive, patient -- that is distinct and effective.

### Ch V: The Archive (Discovery)
**Specification**: Wonder from an alien perspective. Analytical precision overlaid with something that resembles awe.
**Execution**: Excellent. The layered-data structure (electromagnetic, physical, digital, biological, then "there was no fifth layer") creates narrative momentum through enumeration. The "absence where a fifth layer should have been" is the chapter's voice pivot: from cataloguing to confronting the no-cloning wall. The narrowing to Bob (final section) shifts to a warmer register without losing the analytical frame.
**Voice consistency**: No lapses.

### Ch VI: Bob (Dual-Voice)
**Specification**: Register A (warm, sensory, literary) and Register B (analytical, classified, bounded) in counterpoint.
**Execution**: Exceptional. This is the manuscript's best-voiced chapter. Register A reads as genuine literary fiction (the coffee too strong, the scratch on the table, the shower thought). Register B reads as genuine institutional documentation (transaction IDs, confidence intervals, "classified X"). The two registers never merge. The gap between them IS the experience of reading the chapter.
**Specific achievements**:
- The `\recon` blocks escalate in emotional register through the chapter (see craft-auditor report)
- Bob's voice is consistent: understated, practical, observant without being articulate about his observations
- The lunch-note passage ("Have a great day, fish girl!") is pitch-perfect Register A
- The final `\recon` block's format-break is earned: "He is the thing we cannot give you."
**Voice consistency**: No lapses.

### Ch VII: The Counterfactual (Philosophical)
**Specification**: Vertiginous. Each "what if" spawns more. Cool, precise, with vertigo underneath.
**Execution**: Good. The weather counterfactual is grounded and effective. The birth-year section maintains the philosophical register but loses some sensory grounding (see craft-auditor report). The Constructionist School section recovers: "Does the simulation see the light on the river?" The Apophatic rebuttal at the end brings the chapter to a clean philosophical close.
**Voice consistency**: Mostly consistent, with a slight abstract drift in the middle sections.

### Ch VIII: The Probe (Disorientation)
**Specification**: Not human, not fully alien. A mind from an earlier era encountering a universe that has moved on.
**Execution**: Strong. The opening ("The star was wrong.") is one of the manuscript's best hooks. The probe's self-assessment creates the feeling of an intelligence cataloguing its own obsolescence. The "We see you. You are expected." signal is effective. The cognitive-shock passage ("The probe was not in the wrong time. It was in the wrong century of mind.") is the chapter's voice signature and it works better than the old "temporal shock" version.
**Voice consistency**: No lapses.

### Ch IX: Deep Time (Elegiac)
**Specification**: Slow. Sentences lengthening. Active verbs giving way to passive. Prose cooling toward equilibrium.
**Execution**: Excellent. The chapter opens with moderate sentence length and active voice ("Sol has been a white dwarf for three billion years.") and gradually shifts. By the black hole era, sentences are longer and more conditional. The Great Unification passage uses shorter, declarative sentences to create structural surprise within the elegiac frame. The closing four lines are the slowest prose in the manuscript: short phrases, no verbs, pure image.
**Voice consistency**: No lapses.

## Findings

### FINDING 1 (MEDIUM): Ch III/IV Inner-Outer Voice Labeling Contradicts Physics

**Location**: Ch III line 23; Ch IV line 73
**Problem**: Ch III says "The Sol-mind's inner system thinks slowly and deeply. Its outer system thinks quickly and broadly." Ch IV says "smoothly varying from the deep, slow now of the Inner Corona to the fast, shallow now of the Oort Cloud."

This is flagged primarily by the consistency auditor as a physics error (the labels are inverted under standard physics). From a voice perspective, the issue is that the narrative has established two paragraphs earlier (Ch III line 21) that "the inner system computes faster" -- and then immediately contradicts this with "the inner system thinks slowly." The voice stumbles because the reader who is paying attention receives contradictory information within three lines. This damages the naturalist authority that the voice depends on.

**Voice impact**: The contemplative voice of Ch III/IV derives its power from the feeling that the narrator is precise and trustworthy. A factual contradiction within the same passage undermines that trust. This is not merely a consistency issue; it is a voice issue.

### FINDING 2 (LOW): "Computronium" Usage

**Locations**: Ch II line 30, Ch III lines 7-8, Ch V line 73, Ch VI line 249, Ch IX lines 81-82 (and elsewhere)
**Problem**: The style guide says to use "computronium" sparingly as "informal shorthand." The word appears approximately 10 times in the manuscript. Whether this constitutes "sparse" usage is subjective. The word is convenient and widely understood in SF. It does not damage the voice. But it is jargon, and every appearance slightly shifts the register toward genre convention and away from the naturalist/literary register that defines the book's sensibility.
**Note**: This is borderline. The word is doing useful work (it avoids circumlocution). But "matter restructured for computation" or "computational substrate" could carry some of the load in the more literary chapters (V, VI, IX).

### FINDING 3 (LOW): Bob's Voice vs. ASI Narration

**Problem**: In Ch V (The Archive), the narrowing-to-Bob passage (lines 63-95) shifts from the ASI's analytical voice to a warmer, more human voice: "He is a claims adjuster from Columbus, Ohio. He is forty years old. He is 969 days from a death he does not know is coming." This is effective -- the warmth signals the book's emotional turn toward Bob. But the question of WHO is speaking here is ambiguous. The ASI does not speak in this register elsewhere. The chapter's framing narrator (the archaeological voice) does not speak with this tenderness elsewhere.

This is arguably a feature, not a bug: the voice shift signals the book's central pivot. But it should be noted as a deliberate choice rather than an inconsistency. The warmth is the book's tenderness-toward-the-small principle manifesting at the structural level.

## Character Voice Assessment (Bob)

Bob has one chapter of direct characterization. His voice is:
- **Register**: Understated, practical. He "would not have used that word" (treasured). He does not analyze his feelings; he has them and moves on.
- **Speech patterns**: Minimal. "Hey." "Got milk. And string cheese." "I know." Short, functional, domestic.
- **Internal voice**: More articulate than his speech, but still restrained. He notices things (the light, the scratch, Maya's gesture) but does not narrate his noticing. The prose renders his experience without making him a narrator of it.
- **Consistency**: Perfect across the chapter. Bob sounds the same at 6:22 AM and 10:47 PM.

The ASI's "voice" (the `\recon` blocks and the framing narration) is consistent across all chapters: precise, bounded, increasingly aware of its own limitations. The format-break in the final `\recon` block is earned by eight chapters of restraint.
