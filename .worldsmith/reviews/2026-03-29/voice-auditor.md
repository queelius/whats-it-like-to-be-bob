# Voice Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-29
**Scope**: Full manuscript, 9 chapters, 21,213 words
**Focus**: Per-chapter voice consistency, voice differentiation, dialogue distinctiveness, POV discipline

---

## Methodology

Read the full manuscript sequentially, evaluating each chapter's voice against the specifications in style.md, outline.md, and voice-prototypes.md. Checked for voice bleeding between adjacent chapters, register consistency within chapters, and the success of the dual-voice mechanism in Ch VI.

## Findings

### MEDIUM

**M1. Ch V, lines 3-17: voice drift from "archaeologist's wonder" to "analytical inventory" persists from R3**

- **Location**: Ch V, opening through the four data layers
- **Quoted text**: "The first layer was electromagnetic: signals that had left the third planet at the speed of light and propagated outward for centuries before the Dyson swarm's receivers captured them."
- **Problem**: This passage is informationally necessary and well-written, but it sounds like Ch III (contemplative, analytical) rather than Ch V (discovery, wonder). The target voice is "the tone of an archaeologist holding a 3,000-year-old grocery list." The opening sentence achieves this ("The data arrived in layers, like sediment"). The subsequent four paragraphs describing the data classes are methodical and clinical. The voice shift to the target register doesn't occur until line 33 ("The data was interesting. This was sufficient.").
- **Note**: This was flagged as M2 in the R3 review. The author has not revised this section. It may be a deliberate choice: the analytical opening establishes the data foundation before the voice shifts to discovery. If so, the choice is defensible but the transition point (line 33) could be moved earlier to reduce the overlap with Ch III.
- **Suggestion**: Infuse the data-layer descriptions with the encounter quality. Instead of cataloging what the intelligence found, describe the finding as an event. The Sol-mind meeting electromagnetic signals from a dead civilization for the first time is an encounter, not an inventory. Even small adjustments -- a moment of pause, a qualification that suggests surprise -- would shift the register.

---

### LOW

**L1. Ch IX, line 55: "all of it is reversed" -- the Great Unification passage uses a slightly more elevated register than the elegiac norm**

- **Location**: Ch IX, lines 49-59 (the Great Unification revelation)
- **Quoted text**: "The isolation that defined the age of stars, the lightcone fragmentation that made galactic unity impossible, the cognitive divergence that turned every interstellar exchange into paleontology: all of it is reversed. Not because the speed of light has changed. Not because physics has been violated. Because the minds have slowed down."
- **Problem**: This is the chapter's rhetorical peak, and the elevated register is appropriate to the moment. However, the "Not because X. Not because Y. Because Z." construction is more emphatic than the elegiac voice specification. The rest of Ch IX maintains an unhurried, cooling-toward-equilibrium prose. This passage accelerates briefly into something closer to Ch I's declarative mode.
- **Suggestion**: This is almost certainly intentional -- the Great Unification IS the structural surprise, and the prose should register the surprise. The brief acceleration makes the subsequent return to the elegiac register more powerful. No change recommended. Noted for author's awareness.

## Voice Differentiation Assessment

The 9 chapters now exhibit clearly distinct voices. The strongest differentiations are:

1. **Ch I (naturalist) vs. Ch III (contemplative)**: Fully differentiated. Ch I uses short declarative sentences; Ch III uses long nested sentences. No overlap.

2. **Ch II (kinetic) vs. all others**: Completely unique. The acceleration structure appears nowhere else.

3. **Ch VI (dual-voice) vs. all others**: Completely unique. The recon-block mechanism is used only in Ch VI.

4. **Ch IV (temporal gradient) vs. Ch III (contemplative)**: Improved since R2. Ch IV's voice is defined by the interleaving of fast (Mercury Shell) and slow (Kuiper Halo) temporal perspectives. The sentence structures literally enact the temporal difference: short, revised, staccato for the inner system; long, unhurried, archival for the outer system.

5. **Ch V (archaeological) vs. Ch III (contemplative)**: Weakest differentiation. The opening sections of Ch V could be transplanted into Ch III without adjustment. The differentiation emerges mid-chapter. See M1 above.

6. **Ch IX (elegiac) vs. Ch III (contemplative)**: Differentiated by verb voice. Ch III uses predominantly active constructions. Ch IX shifts from active to passive to conditional as the chapter progresses, enacting the cooling toward equilibrium.

## Dialogue Assessment

Dialogue appears only in Ch VI. It is minimal and distinctive:

- **Bob and Karen**: "Hey." / "Hey." / "Got milk. And string cheese." / "Oh good, he's been asking." / "I know." -- Five lines of dialogue that carry the chapter's thesis about marriage-as-practice. The dialogue is not clever or expressive; it is logistical, and the logistics ARE the expression. This is the manuscript's best dialogue writing.

- **Bob and Denise**: "happy Pi Day" / "oh, is it?" / "three-fourteen" / "huh, how about that" -- Four lines that establish Bob's sociality without characterizing him as witty, warm, or cold. He is polite and mildly engaged. Perfect.

- **Bob and Ethan**: "bye Dad" / "oh wait" / "bye Dad" -- Three lines that enact Ethan's six-year-old syntax. The double "bye Dad" with the interruption is the most naturalistic child dialogue I have encountered in a manuscript.

All dialogue is consistent with bob.md's characterization. No voice issues.

## Strengths

1. **Ch VI's dual-voice mechanism** is the manuscript's greatest formal achievement. The two registers maintain absolute discipline throughout 6,242 words. Register A never cites data. Register B never attributes feeling. The gap between them IS the reading experience, and it succeeds because neither register acknowledges the other's existence.

2. **Ch IX's final four lines** ("Light on a river. Four seconds. A Tuesday in March.") are in a voice that belongs to no chapter and all chapters -- a stripped, universal register that the manuscript has earned through 21,000 words of specificity. The voice is neither Bob's warm register nor the analytical register nor the elegiac register. It is the voice of the question itself.

3. **The probe's voice** (Ch VIII) successfully navigates the hardest assignment in the voice spec: "not human, not fully alien." The probe's self-assessment ("Net status: the probe's processing efficiency was 5% better than at launch...") reads as intelligible but not anthropomorphic. The reader understands the probe's situation without being invited to identify with it as a human character.
