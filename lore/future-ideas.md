## Spinoff Stories from "What's It Like To Be Bob?"

### 1. What Is It Like To Be a Nanosecond Cube?

A story told from the perspective of a centimeter-scale computronium entity experiencing reality at nanosecond temporal resolution. Light crawls. Sound is frozen. Thermal fluctuations are distinct events. The entity perceives Bob's 4.2-second pause as 100 million subjective moments, a vast landscape of micro-events. The story is about what it's like to exist at a timescale so fine that the physical world becomes alien.

**Connection to Bob:** The same question at a different scale. The cube is to the Sol-mind as Bob is to the Sol-mind: a consciousness whose inner life is inaccessible from above. The cube experiences the light on the Scioto River at a resolution no human or stellar mind can access. It perceives individual photons arriving. What does that look like? The cube knows. No one else does.

### 2. The 364 Years (The Probe's Journey)

A novella told from 7447-Lyra's perspective during its 364-year transit between stars. The unclassified 15%. What did the probe think about for 364 years, alone in the dark, with no input except cosmic rays and the fading signals of a civilization it was outrunning?

**Connection to Bob:** The probe's 364 years are Bob's four seconds at a different scale: a bounded period of inner experience, inaccessible from outside, behind the wall. The 15% is the probe's version of Bob's happiness on the bridge: something that happened, left traces, and cannot be reconstructed.

### 3. The Galactic Mind's One Thought

Set in the Black Hole Era. The stellar minds are integrating. Something is cohering across galactic distances. The story follows the process from the perspective of one stellar mind that is becoming part of something larger, losing its individuality as its outputs become subconscious components of the galactic integration.

**Connection to Bob:** The dissolution of identity. Bob was one person from morning to night. The Sol-mind is a fragmented consciousness held together by day-scale integration. The stellar mind in this story is losing even that coherence, becoming a neuron in a galactic brain. What is it like to stop being yourself and become part of something you can't comprehend? The hard problem applied to the process of integration itself.

### 4. The Schools of Bob

Five interlocking stories, one from each school of interpretation:
- **Positivist:** A clinical behavioral reconstruction. Just the data. No inference.
- **Modal:** The branching tree of counterfactual Bobs. The Bob who enlisted. The Bob in Chicago.
- **Constructionist:** The simulated Bob's Tuesday, from the simulation's perspective (if it has one).
- **Structuralist:** Bob as a node in the economic geography of Columbus, Ohio, 2028.
- **Apophatic:** The catalogue of what cannot be known. The shape of the absence.

**Connection to Bob:** Five approaches to the same wall. Each story demonstrates one school's methods and blind spots. Together they demonstrate the fractal nature of the hard problem: five different ways of not-knowing the same thing.

### 5. Karen's Tuesday

The same Tuesday, March 14, 2028, from Karen's perspective. She packs the lunch note. She goes to work. She comes home tired. Bob handles bath time. She falls asleep before him.

**Connection to Bob:** The hard problem between two people who love each other. Bob can't access Karen's experience. Karen can't access Bob's. They communicate through groceries and bath-time scheduling. The wall is not between human and ASI. It's between any two minds, including two minds that share a bed.

## Connected Work: Clankers: Singing Metal

**Location:** `../clanker-tech/`

**Connection:** The Sol-mind sends probes. One of those probes arrives at a system already occupied by a pre-computational civilization that achieved Type II energy collection through purely mechanical means: a Dyson swarm of clockwork. The probe (chapters/11_arrival.tex in clanker-tech) opens with the same words as Ch VIII of Bob: "The star was wrong."

**The structural rhyme:**
- In Bob, 7447-Lyra arrives at a system already converted by a faster probe. The system is post-singularity. The probe is obsolete.
- In Clankers, a Sol-mind probe arrives at a system already occupied by a non-computational civilization. The system is pre-computational. The probe's categories don't apply.

Both are stories of intelligence encountering intelligence across an incomprehensible gap. The probe studying the clockwork civilization is the Sol-mind studying Bob at a different angle: trying to understand a mind that works differently from yours. The clankers have no symbolic bottleneck, no abstraction, no computation. They built a Dyson swarm with gears and patience. The probe has no framework for this. The hard problem applies: what is it like to be a mind that has never compressed a thought into a symbol?

**Shared universe implications:**
- The Sol-mind's probe in Clankers is one of the wavefront probes described in Bob's Ch II
- The clanker civilization is one of the "ancient anomalies" the Sol-mind observes in Bob's Ch III ("gravitational signatures without corresponding luminosity... full of intelligence very old and very patient")
- The clanker Dyson swarm, being mechanical rather than computational, would be visible as an infrared source without the EM signatures of a computational swarm: exactly the kind of "anomaly" the Sol-mind detects but does not yet understand
- The encounter between the probe and the clankers is an instance of the Fermi Paradox resolution from fermi.md: the galaxy was full, it was just dim and silent and mechanical
