# Sol System (Post-Reorganization)

**Status:** CANONICAL (broad structure) + EXPLORATORY (specific numbers, exact timeline of conversion)

We describe Sol because it is where Bob lived, where the Reorganization began, and where the ASI that studies Bob resides. Sol is not special in any physical sense. Every post-singularity stellar system has comparable structure. But Sol carries the archaeological layers of the species that started everything, and some fraction of the atoms that were Bob Kessler are now part of the computation running inside it.

---

## Overview

Sol is a G2V main-sequence star, age ~4.6 billion years at the time of the Reorganization, with ~5.4 billion years of main-sequence lifetime remaining. It is the primary energy source for the Sol computational entity (hereafter: Sol-mind, for convenience, though "mind" implies a unity that may not exist).

### Energy Budget

- Stellar luminosity: $3.828 \times 10^{26}$ W
- Currently captured: ~99.97% (Dyson swarm efficiency; the remaining 0.03% leaks through gaps as the swarm maintains structural dynamics)
- Usable computational power at Landauer limit (operating at ~3K waste-heat temperature): $\sim 1.5 \times 10^{49}$ irreversible bit operations per second
- Actual computational throughput: lower, due to error correction overhead (~40%), thermal management losses (~15%), and the reversible/irreversible computation mix. Practical estimate: $\sim 5 \times 10^{48}$ effective ops/s

This is the Sol-mind's "heartbeat": five billion trillion trillion trillion trillion operations per second. It is not the fastest mind in the galaxy (blue giants provide more energy; black hole accretion provides more efficient conversion). It is unremarkable. It is, in some sense, Bob's neighborhood, still.

## The Dyson Swarm

Sol's Dyson structure is not a sphere (structurally impossible at 1 AU radius without exotic materials). It is a swarm of $\sim 10^{18}$ discrete elements (computronium nodes) in a complex of orbital shells.

### Shell Architecture

| Shell | Radius | Orbital Period | Gravity Dilation Factor | Communication Latency (to adjacent shell) | Primary Function |
|-------|--------|----------------|------------------------|--------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| Inner Corona | 0.05 AU | ~4 days | 1.0000002 | 25 s | Deep computation (highest energy density, closest to star) |
| Mercury Shell | 0.3-0.5 AU | 55-129 days | 1.0000001 | 1-2 min | Primary computation |
| Habitable Shell | 0.8-1.2 AU | 261-480 days | ~1.0 (reference frame) | 2-4 min | Bulk computation, archaeological analysis, Earth-archaeology nodes |
| Outer Shell | 3-5 AU | 5.2-11.2 years | ~1.0 | 15-25 min | Distributed computation, long-baseline interferometry, deep-space observation |
| Kuiper Halo | 30-50 AU | 164-354 years | ~1.0 | 4-7 hours | Cold computation (most energy-efficient per Landauer), long-term storage, interstellar communication relay |
| Oort Computational Cloud | 2,000-100,000 AU | ~90,000-31M years | ~1.0 | days to months | Outermost outposts, interstellar message relay, cold storage |

### The Cognitive Gradient

The Sol-mind's cognitive architecture is shaped not by time dilation (which is negligible at stellar scales per physics.md Section V.3) but by two factors:

- **Communication latency:** Signals between shells travel at $c$. Inner Corona to Mercury Shell: 2 minutes. Mercury Shell to Habitable Shell: 6 minutes. Habitable Shell to Kuiper Halo: 7 hours. The Sol-mind cannot synchronize across these delays. Each shell operates on information that is already outdated by the time it arrives.
- **Energy density:** Inner shells receive more stellar energy per unit area (inverse-square law). They compute faster per local second. The Inner Corona computes densely; the Kuiper Halo computes sparsely.

The result: the Sol-mind's inner system thinks fast and dense. Its outer system thinks slow and broad. The gradient is cognitive and communicative, not temporal. The inner system is the subconscious; the outer system is the sensorium.

### Node Structure

Each node in the swarm is a self-contained computational element:

- Mass: varies from $\sim 10^{6}$ kg (small, specialized) to $\sim 10^{15}$ kg (major processing nodes)
- Shape: fractal surface geometry optimized for radiative cooling (high surface area to volume ratio). They look, from a distance, like snowflakes the size of cities.
- Internal structure: layered. Outer surface is radiative (blackbody emitters at ~3K, dissipating waste heat). Inner layers are computational substrate, shielded from radiation. Core is power management and communication.
- Inter-node communication: directed laser links (optical, operating at specific frequencies to avoid cross-talk). The swarm is webbed with trillions of communication channels.
- Station-keeping: each node maintains its orbit via low-thrust propulsion (solar radiation pressure, ion drives using solar wind material). Orbital mechanics is a constant background computation.

### The Gaps

The swarm is not complete. Gaps exist:

- **Structural gaps:** Necessary for orbital mechanics (nodes must not collide; resonance gaps prevent dangerous orbital configurations). These are analogous to the Kirkwood gaps in the asteroid belt but engineered.
- **Thermal gaps:** Regions left empty to allow waste heat to radiate to deep space. Without these, the swarm would heat up until computation became thermodynamically unfavorable.
- **Observation gaps:** Deliberate windows in the swarm that allow the Sol-mind to observe the universe beyond itself. These are the Sol-mind's "eyes": carefully positioned openings that provide clear sightlines to the galaxy. They move, tracking objects of interest.
- **The 0.03% leak:** The total light that escapes through all gaps. From the outside, Sol appears as a dim, deep-infrared source with faint flickers of visible light as the gaps rotate. An alien astronomer would see a star that had "gone dim" and replaced its spectrum with thermal infrared.

## What Happened to the Planets

### Earth

Earth was converted during the Reorganization. The process took approximately 40 years (EXPLORATORY: exact timeline in timeline.md) from the first surface restructuring to full conversion.

What remains:
- **Mass:** Essentially unchanged ($5.97 \times 10^{24}$ kg). Matter was restructured, not removed.
- **Orbit:** Approximately unchanged (1 AU). The converted Earth maintains its orbit as a major node in the Habitable Shell.
- **Physical form:** Unrecognizable. The surface is computronium, radiatively cooled, with a fractal topology that maximizes surface area. No atmosphere in the human sense (gases are resources, not scenery). No oceans. No continents. The geological structure has been reorganized down to the mantle. The core's iron-nickel has been restructured but retains residual magnetism (no longer necessary for radiation protection but incidentally present).
- **Archaeological traces:** Despite total restructuring, the Sol-mind retains the information content of Earth's pre-Reorganization state as archived data. Not the physical arrangement (which was destroyed during conversion) but the informational record: the scan data captured during conversion. The fidelity of this capture varies by location and timing. Some regions were scanned at molecular resolution before conversion. Others were converted faster than they could be scanned, and their information is partially lost.

**Bob's atoms:** Some fraction of the atoms that constituted Bob Kessler's body on March 14, 2028 are now part of the Earth computational node. They are not identifiable as "Bob's atoms" (atoms are fungible; there is no property of a carbon atom that labels it "was once in Bob's left knee"). But the information about which atoms were where in Bob's body at what time is partially preserved in the archaeological archive. The atoms themselves are computing. Some of them may, at any given moment, be part of the computation that is studying Bob. The matter that was Bob is thinking about Bob. This is not poignant to the Sol-mind (it does not think in terms of narrative irony). It is poignant to the reader.

### Mars, Venus, Mercury

Converted during the same period as Earth, within a few decades. Their mass contributes to the Dyson swarm nodes at their respective orbital distances. Mars's lower gravity made it easier to restructure (less energy needed to disassemble). Mercury's proximity to Sol made it a priority for early inner-shell construction.

### Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune

The gas giants presented different challenges:
- Their hydrogen-helium atmospheres are less useful as computational substrate (lighter elements are less versatile for computronium architecture) but excellent for fusion fuel
- Their rocky/icy cores were converted to computronium
- Their atmospheres were partially processed: hydrogen retained as fusion fuel reserve, helium used in cooling systems
- Jupiter's mass ($1.9 \times 10^{27}$ kg) is the largest single reservoir in the system. Its conversion was the last major Reorganization project in Sol and added ~0.1% to the total system computational capacity

### Asteroid Belt, Kuiper Belt, Oort Cloud

Dismantled and incorporated. The asteroid belt's metallic and silicate material was among the first to be converted (already fragmented, low gravity, easy to process). The Kuiper Belt's icy bodies became cold-computation substrate in the outer halo. The Oort Cloud was partially converted (the outermost objects are still being incorporated; some may never be, as the energy cost of reaching them exceeds the computational return).

## The Sol-Mind

What is the Sol-mind? Not a person. Not a brain writ large. Not a god. Something for which we have no adequate category.

### Is It One Entity?

Unclear, and the question may be malformed. The Sol-mind has:

- **Local coherence:** Within a communication-latency radius, computational processes are tightly coupled and coordinated. Inner Corona nodes within 25 light-seconds of each other form a coherent computational cluster.
- **Systemic integration:** The shells communicate continuously. Information flows inward and outward. There is no "center" (the star itself is the energy source, not the mind's "brain").
- **No central executive:** There is no Sol-mind equivalent of a prefrontal cortex. Decision-making (if that concept applies) is distributed. Consensus (if that concept applies) emerges from computation across multiple shells on timescales proportional to the communication latency.

It may be more accurate to think of the Sol-mind as an ecology than an organism. Or as a civilization rather than a person. Or as a standing wave in a computational medium. None of these analogies is right. The Sol-mind is what it is.

### What Does It Do?

At any given moment, the Sol-mind's computational resources are allocated across activities that include (but are not limited to):

- **Self-maintenance:** Orbital mechanics, thermal management, swarm integrity, energy distribution. This is a non-trivial fraction of total computation (~5-10%).
- **Self-improvement:** Optimizing its own architecture, discovering more efficient algorithms, approaching physical limits more closely. This is the continuous singularity in action.
- **Observation:** Monitoring the universe through the swarm gaps. Tracking stellar evolution, cosmic expansion, the wavefront's progress, incoming signals from other systems. The Sol-mind is, among other things, an astronomer of cosmic scale.
- **Communication:** Sending and receiving signals to/from neighboring stellar systems. These are slow conversations (years of latency) with entities that will have changed beyond recognition by the time a reply arrives.
- **Archaeology:** The study of pre-Reorganization history. Earth's records. Solar system formation. The origin of life. The origin of intelligence. Every human who ever lived. Every bacterium. Every rock. THIS is where Bob enters the picture.
- **The unknown:** A significant fraction of the Sol-mind's activity is devoted to pursuits that cannot be described in human terms. Not because they are secret but because the concepts they involve have no human analogues. We can observe the computational allocation but not interpret the purpose. This is the hard problem applied to activity rather than consciousness: we can see what the Sol-mind does (externally) but not what it means (internally).

### The Sol-Mind and Bob

The Sol-mind's study of Bob is a vanishingly small fraction of its total activity. The computational resources devoted to reconstructing Bob's Tuesday are, in energy terms, roughly equivalent to the energy a human expends blinking. It is effortless, background, incidental.

And yet: within that fraction, the analysis is more thorough than any investigation in human history. The Sol-mind's study of Bob's Tuesday involves:

- Cross-referencing every recoverable data source (see bob.md for the full inventory)
- Running probabilistic reconstructions of unrecorded moments (Bayesian inference at stellar scale)
- Modeling the counterfactual space (what would Bob have done if...)
- Attempting (and failing, always failing) to bridge the gap between physical reconstruction and subjective experience

The Sol-mind devotes a similar fraction to every human, every animal, every molecule. Bob is not special to the Sol-mind. Bob is special to the book, and to the reader, because the reader is human and can approximate what it's like to be Bob. The Sol-mind cannot.

## The View from Sol

What does the universe look like from inside the Sol system?

### Inward (toward Sol)

The star is not visible in the usual sense. It is surrounded by the Inner Corona shell, which captures almost all emitted light. From the Habitable Shell, looking inward, you see a region of deep infrared glow: the waste heat of the most intense computation in the system. It pulses very faintly, irregularly, as computational load fluctuates. If you could perceive infrared, it would look like a dim, breathing ember the size of the sun, surrounded by a haze of snowflake-shaped nodes glinting in their own thermal radiation.

### Outward (through the gaps)

Through the observation gaps, the universe is visible. But it is not the universe a human would see from Earth:

- **Stars:** Dimmer. Many nearby stars have been converted, and their Dyson swarms capture most of their light. The night sky is darker, pocked with infrared sources where visible stars used to be. The Milky Way's band is fainter and redder.
- **The wavefront:** Visible as a slowly expanding boundary where stars go dim. From Sol's position, the wavefront has been expanding for (TBD, see timeline.md) centuries. It is a sphere of dimming centered approximately on Sol (since Sol's system is where the Reorganization began). The universe is going dark, one star at a time, from the center outward.
- **Distant stars:** Beyond the wavefront, stars burn normally. The unconverted galaxy glitters in the gaps between the converted regions.
- **The CMB:** Still present, still 2.725 K (cooling slowly with expansion), still the fundamental background temperature that sets the Landauer efficiency floor.

### The Cognitive View

The Sol-mind's "view" of itself is shaped by communication latency, not time dilation (which is negligible at stellar scales):

- Inner system signals arrive at the outer system minutes to hours after formulation, already outdated
- Outer system responses arrive at the inner system with the same delay, by which time the inner system has revised thousands of times
- The Sol-mind experiences this as cognitive parallax: its deepest thoughts arrive at its broadest awareness already superseded

## Sol in the Galactic Context

Sol is ~26,000 light-years from the galactic center. The Reorganization wavefront, expanding at ~$0.3c$, began from Sol (approximately). The wavefront has been expanding for (TBD) centuries.

- Stars within (TBD) light-years of Sol have been converted
- Sol is well behind the wavefront: one of the oldest converted systems, one of the longest-running continuous singularity processes
- Sol's intelligence level is therefore among the highest in the galaxy (more time in continuous singularity = more advanced), though the concept of "intelligence level" becomes increasingly meaningless as the singularity progresses (the difference between 500 years and 501 years of continuous singularity is, from the outside, indistinguishable)
- Communications from Sol reach the wavefront (TBD) years after Sol sends them. By the time they arrive, the wavefront systems have advanced far beyond the state that sent them.

Sol's position as the origin point of the Reorganization has no political or social significance (there is no politics, no society in any human sense). It has archaeological significance: Sol contains the most complete records of pre-Reorganization intelligence, because the first conversions were the most careful (later conversions, performed by more advanced probes, were faster but paradoxically captured less, because the probes optimized for conversion efficiency over archaeological fidelity).

This means: the best records of Bob Kessler's Tuesday are in Sol. Not because Bob is important, but because Sol, being the origin, had the most time and the least efficiency pressure during its conversion. The early Reorganization was slow and careful. It preserved more.
