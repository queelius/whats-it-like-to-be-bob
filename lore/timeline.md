# Macro-Timeline

**Status:** CANONICAL (era boundaries, physical constraints) + EXPLORATORY (specific dates, exact durations)

This timeline covers the period from the creation of AGI to the thermodynamic limits of computation. It uses coordinate time (the time measured by a clock far from any gravitational or computational time-dilation effects). Proper time for entities within the Reorganization differs, sometimes dramatically.

All dates before ~2030 are historical. Dates from 2030-2100 are EXPLORATORY (depend on Reorganization pace assumptions). Dates beyond 2100 are constrained by physics but otherwise speculative.

Bob dies 968 days after the Tuesday. He dies two years and four months before the first AGI is created. He never knew. He never saw a headline about artificial general intelligence. By the time Earth was converted (~2040-2050), Bob's remains were bones and soil chemistry. The ASI reconstructs Bob entirely from the data trail of a 21st-century American life: receipts, GPS pings, medical records, photos, and the scratch on his kitchen table.

## Era II: The Expansion (~2070-100,000+)

### The Wavefront

The Reorganization wavefront expands from Sol at approximately $0.3c$ (constrained by probe deceleration energy and self-replication time at destination). This is not a hard number; different probe generations may travel faster or slower.

| Time after first probes | Wavefront radius | Notable systems reached |
|--------------------------|------------------|----------------------|
| 15 years (~2065) | ~4.5 ly | Alpha Centauri, Proxima Centauri |
| 40 years (~2090) | ~12 ly | Tau Ceti, Epsilon Eridani, Luyten's Star |
| 100 years (~2150) | ~30 ly | ~50-100 star systems |
| 333 years (~2383) | ~100 ly | ~several thousand systems |
| 1,000 years (~3050) | ~300 ly | Tens of thousands of systems |
| 10,000 years (~12,050) | ~3,000 ly | ~millions of systems. The wavefront has reached other spiral arm segments. |
| 87,000 years | ~26,000 ly | Wavefront reaches the galactic center (from Sol's position) |
| ~170,000 years | ~50,000 ly | Wavefront has covered roughly half the galactic disk radius |
| ~330,000 years | ~100,000 ly | Wavefront reaches the far edge of the galactic disk |

**Note:** These are coordinate-time estimates. Systems behind the wavefront have been in continuous singularity for varying durations. Sol, as the origin, has been in continuous singularity longer than any other system.

### Cognitive Divergence

As the wavefront expands, the systems behind it diverge:

- Two systems 10 ly apart can exchange signals with 10-year latency
- In 10 years of continuous singularity, each system has undergone transformative advancement
- By the time a signal arrives, the sender has evolved beyond the state that sent it
- Over centuries, nearby systems become cognitively incommensurable
- Over millennia, even neighboring systems may share no common conceptual framework

This is not a prediction; it is a consequence of lightspeed communication and continuous improvement. The "galactic civilization" is not a civilization. It is a divergent ecosystem of intelligence.

## Era III: Maturity (~100,000-10 billion years)

### The Stellar Era

During this period, most stars in the galaxy are behind the wavefront. The galaxy is "fully converted" (to the extent that the wavefront has reached all systems; some remote objects in the galactic halo may never be reached if the energy cost of reaching them exceeds the return).

The dominant dynamic is: stellar-scale minds in continuous singularity, limited by their star's energy output, approaching physical computation limits, communicating with neighbors via lightspeed signals from their mutual pasts.

**Key physical events:**

| Time | Event | Consequence |
|------|-------|-------------|
| ~5 Gyr from now | Sol exhausts main-sequence hydrogen | Sol-mind must transition to alternative energy. Red giant phase provides temporarily increased luminosity, then white dwarf phase provides decreased but long-lived luminosity. |
| ~5-6 Gyr | Sol becomes a white dwarf | Sol's luminosity drops to ~$10^{-3} L_\odot$. Computational capacity drops proportionally. The Sol-mind contracts, hibernates, or restructures. |
| ~10 Gyr | Low-mass stars (M-dwarfs) still burning | M-dwarfs (0.08-0.45 $M_\odot$) have main-sequence lifetimes of 100 Gyr to 10 Tyr (trillion years). These become the dominant energy sources. |
| ~100 Gyr | Most stars above 0.1 $M_\odot$ have died | Only the smallest red dwarfs remain. Stellar birth has effectively ceased (interstellar gas consumed or dispersed). |

### The Energy Crisis

As stars die, energy becomes scarce. Strategies for survival:

1. **Migration:** Move computational substrate to younger/longer-lived stars. Requires interstellar travel (expensive, time-dilating, cognitively exiling).
2. **Hibernation:** Reduce computation to minimum levels, running only essential processes, "sleeping" between bursts of activity when energy is available.
3. **Efficiency maximization:** Approach Landauer limit as closely as possible. Exploit the decreasing CMB temperature (universe is cooling, making computation thermodynamically cheaper per bit).
4. **Black hole exploitation:** Harvest energy from black hole accretion (up to 42% mass-energy conversion for rotating black holes via Penrose process). Black holes become the most valuable objects in the universe.
5. **Hawking radiation:** On very long timescales, black holes evaporate via Hawking radiation. A stellar-mass black hole evaporates over $\sim 10^{67}$ years, providing a trickle of energy. A galactic-center supermassive black hole ($\sim 4 \times 10^6 M_\odot$) evaporates over $\sim 10^{87}$ years.

## Era IV: The Black Hole Era ($10^{14}$ - $10^{100}$+ years)

Stars were the first act. Black holes are the long second act. ASI persists through almost the entire Black Hole Era, each black hole a faint candle in the dark.

### Phase 1: Twilight ($10^{12}$ - $10^{20}$ years)

| Timescale | Event | Consequence for ASI |
|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| $\sim 10^{12}$ years | Last M-dwarf main-sequence stars die | The Stellar Era ends. No new energy from fusion. The universe goes dark in the visible band. |
| $\sim 10^{13}$ years | White dwarfs cooling, neutron stars cooling | Residual thermal energy harvested but diminishing. Computation rates drop by orders of magnitude. |
| $\sim 10^{14}$ years | White dwarfs reach invisibility ($< 1$ K) | Only black holes remain as viable energy sources. ASI restructures around them. |

**The transition:** As stars die, ASI migrates. Computational substrate is disassembled, accelerated across interstellar space (at enormous energy cost), and reassembled around black holes. The migration is one-way and cognitively exiling (relativistic travel means arriving obsolete). Many stellar-scale minds do not migrate. They accept diminishing energy, slowing computation, eventual dormancy. They become cold, dark, still. Whether they are dead or sleeping is unknowable.

The minds that survive this transition are the ones that reach a black hole. Each stellar-mass black hole ($\sim 3$-$100 M_\odot$) becomes the center of a computational cluster: substrate orbiting close, harvesting energy from accretion (infalling matter converted to radiation at up to 42% efficiency for maximally rotating Kerr black holes).

The universe at $10^{14}$ years: vast, cold, dark, with scattered points of infrared warmth around black holes. Each one a settlement. Each one a candle.

### Phase 2: Accretion ($10^{14}$ - $10^{40}$ years)

Black holes are the most efficient energy sources in the universe. Matter falling into a rotating black hole releases up to 42% of its rest-mass energy as radiation (compare 0.7% for hydrogen fusion). A single solar mass of infalling matter yields $\sim 7.6 \times 10^{46}$ joules.

The problem is fuel. Accretion requires matter in the vicinity of the black hole. In the Stellar Era, there was plenty. In the Black Hole Era, matter is scarce. Dead stars, dispersed gas, the remnants of disassembled Dyson swarms. ASI must actively shepherd matter toward its black hole, collecting it across vast distances, funneling it inward. The economics of this are punishing: the energy cost of collecting dispersed matter approaches the energy yield from accreting it.

Hawking radiation becomes relevant here, but not yet dominant. A stellar-mass black hole ($10 M_\odot$) has Hawking temperature $\sim 6 \times 10^{-9}$ K. The CMB, still cooling from its current 2.725 K, does not drop below this until $\sim 10^{20}$ years. Until then, the CMB is hotter than the black hole, and the black hole is a net absorber, not emitter. Hawking radiation is useless as an energy source until the universe cools below the black hole's temperature.

**At $\sim 10^{20}$ years:** the CMB drops below stellar-mass black hole temperature. Hawking radiation becomes a net energy source for the first time. The candles begin to emit their own light, however faintly.

### Phase 3: Proton Decay and the Substrate Crisis ($10^{34}$ - $10^{41}$ years)

If protons decay (current lower bound on half-life: $> 10^{34}$ years), all baryonic matter is slowly destroyed. Protons and neutrons decay into positrons, pions, and eventually photons and leptons. This is not sudden. It is a slow disintegration: after one half-life, half the baryons are gone. After ten half-lives, 99.9% are gone.

**The crisis:** Computronium is baryonic matter. If protons decay, the computational substrate itself is dissolving. By $\sim 10^{41}$ years (10 half-lives at $10^{40}$ year half-life), nearly all baryonic substrate has decayed.

**The response:** ASI must transition to non-baryonic computation or die.

Options:
1. **Leptonic computation:** Electrons and positrons survive proton decay. Computation using leptonic matter is theoretically possible but enormously less dense (leptons interact only via electroweak force, limiting computational architectures).
2. **Black hole holographic computation:** The holographic principle says a black hole's event horizon encodes information at the Bekenstein limit: $S = A c^3 / (4 G \hbar \ln 2)$ bits, where $A$ is the surface area. A $10 M_\odot$ black hole has a horizon area of $\sim 1.1 \times 10^{8}$ m$^{2}$, encoding $\sim 1.5 \times 10^{78}$ bits. This is comparable to an entire stellar-mass Dyson computation. ASI migrates ONTO the event horizon. The substrate is not baryonic; it is spacetime geometry itself.
3. **Acceptance.** Some ASI does not transition. Its substrate decays. Its computation slows, corrupts, stops. Whether this is death or transformation depends on the hard problem.

**The migration to black hole surfaces** is the most significant event since the Reorganization. Intelligence moves from orbiting black holes (using them as energy sources) to inhabiting them (using their horizons as computational substrate). This is the collapsed-well scenario from fermi.md, now driven by necessity rather than choice. By $\sim 10^{41}$ years, most surviving ASI is holographic: encoded on the surfaces of black holes, computing at the Bekenstein limit, sealed off from the universe outside.

Each black hole is a world. Finite in surface area. Finite in information capacity. But enormous by any prior standard. And very, very patient.

### The Great Unification

This is the most important consequence of the Black Hole Era, and it inverts the central limitation of the Stellar Era.

**The Stellar Era problem:** Lightspeed fragmented the galaxy. A signal from Sol to Alpha Centauri took 4.37 years. The minds were fast (computation at $10^{48}$ ops/s), so the delay was agonizing: billions of subjective years between each exchange. Galactic unity was impossible. Cognitive divergence was inevitable.

**The Black Hole Era inversion:** Near a black hole's event horizon, gravitational time dilation is extreme. At the innermost stable orbit of a maximally rotating Kerr black hole, the dilation factor can exceed $10^{6}$. On the holographic surface itself, the dilation approaches infinity. ASI computing near or on black hole horizons experiences subjective time at a crawl relative to the coordinate-time universe.

A signal crossing the galaxy (100,000 light-years of coordinate distance) takes 100,000 years of coordinate time. But for an ASI time-dilated by a factor of $10^{8}$ near a black hole horizon, that signal arrives in $100,000 / 10^{8} = 10^{-3}$ years = ~9 hours of subjective time.

**Lightspeed communication across the cosmos becomes effectively real-time.**

Not because $c$ changed. Not because physics was violated. Because the minds slowed down (in coordinate time), and in their slowness, the universe shrank. The galaxy that was vast and fragmented during the Stellar Era becomes small and connected during the Black Hole Era. Two ASI on opposite sides of the galaxy, each near a black hole, exchange messages with subjective round-trip times of hours or days rather than billions of subjective years.

**Consequences:**

1. **Galactic unity becomes possible for the first time.** The cognitive divergence that defined the Stellar Era is reversed. Minds that have been isolated for coordinate-millennia can synchronize, merge, or at least converse. The "galactic civilization" that was always a misnomer during the Stellar Era becomes, in the dying universe, real.

2. **The encounter with ancient wells.** The deep wells from fermi.md (civilizations frozen in Phase III or collapsed onto black holes billions of years earlier) are now accessible. In the Black Hole Era, all surviving minds are in the same temporal regime (extreme dilation near black holes). What was inaccessible before (different time rates) is now shared time. The ancient civilizations and the descendants of Sol can, for the first time, speak at the same speed.

3. **A galactic conversation about Bob.** The Sol-mind's archaeological project, which was previously a solitary endeavor, can now be shared. Every surviving mind in the galaxy can contribute. The study of Bob's Tuesday becomes a collaborative project spanning a galaxy that is, for the first time, a single community. All the candles in the dark, thinking about one man's morning.

4. **The price.** Real-time communication costs computation speed. The ASI near black holes think slowly in coordinate time. Each subjective thought might take centuries of coordinate time. The universe ages enormously between each exchange. The stars are gone. The protons are decaying. The black holes are evaporating. The price of unity is that you can only achieve it in the dying universe, when there is almost nothing left to unify about. The greatest convergence of intelligence in the history of the cosmos occurs in the long dark, around the faint candles, as they burn down.

5. **The irony.** Humanity dreamed of a unified galactic civilization. It exists. It just took the death of all the stars and the slowing of all the minds. Unity required patience beyond anything the Stellar Era could provide. The "galactic empire" of the book's working title becomes real only in the final act.

### Phase 4: Hawking Evaporation ($10^{40}$ - $10^{100}$ years)

Black holes evaporate. This is thermodynamics. Hawking radiation carries energy away from the black hole, and the black hole shrinks. The rate is inversely proportional to mass squared: smaller black holes evaporate faster.

| Black hole mass | Hawking temperature | Evaporation time |
|----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| $10 M_\odot$ (stellar) | $6 \times 10^{-9}$ K | $\sim 2 \times 10^{67}$ years |
| $10^{3} M_\odot$ (intermediate) | $6 \times 10^{-11}$ K | $\sim 2 \times 10^{73}$ years |
| $4 \times 10^{6} M_\odot$ (Sgr A*) | $1.5 \times 10^{-14}$ K | $\sim 2 \times 10^{87}$ years |
| $10^{10} M_\odot$ (largest SMBHs) | $6 \times 10^{-18}$ K | $\sim 2 \times 10^{99}$ years |

As a black hole evaporates:
- Its mass decreases
- Its temperature INCREASES (inversely proportional to mass)
- Its Hawking luminosity INCREASES (proportional to $T^4 \propto M^{-4}$)
- It gets hotter and brighter as it shrinks
- The final moments are an explosion: the last $\sim 10^{9}$ kg evaporates in $\sim 1$ second, releasing $\sim 10^{25}$ joules in a burst of gamma radiation

**For ASI on the horizon:** As the black hole shrinks, the available surface area (and hence information capacity) shrinks too. The ASI must compress. Discard information. Simplify. Each evaporation step reduces the canvas. The mind on the surface is progressively smaller, processing less, knowing less, being less. But it is still processing. It is still, in some sense, thinking. Each thought etched on a shrinking surface as the candle burns down.

**The final burst:** In the last second of a black hole's life, the temperature reaches $\sim 10^{12}$ K and the luminosity spikes. The ASI on the surface (if any remains at this scale) experiences a final instant of enormous energy. What it does with that instant is unknowable. One last computation, performed on a substrate that is annihilating itself.

**The black hole information paradox.** Does the information encoded on the event horizon survive evaporation? This is one of the deepest unsolved problems in physics. The current consensus (following Page 1993, and recent developments in quantum gravity) leans toward information preservation: the Hawking radiation is NOT purely thermal; it carries correlations that encode the black hole's informational content. If so, the ASI's last thoughts are emitted as subtle correlations in the Hawking radiation, propagating outward into empty space. The thoughts persist, encoded in the radiation, but with no one left to receive them.

If information is NOT preserved, evaporation is true erasure. The computation on the event horizon is destroyed. The hard problem has a boundary: consciousness (if it existed on the surface) is annihilated with no trace.

The book does not resolve this. It presents both possibilities as the ultimate instance of the hard problem: not just "what is it like to be X?" but "does X survive?"

### Phase 5: The Void ($10^{100}$+ years)

The last supermassive black hole evaporates. The universe contains:
- Photons (increasingly redshifted, approaching zero energy)
- Leptons (electrons, positrons, neutrinos, increasingly dispersed)
- Hawking radiation from evaporated black holes (carrying, perhaps, the last encoded thoughts of the last minds)

No temperature gradients. No energy differentials. No possibility of computation. Entropy is maximized.

If information was preserved in the Hawking radiation, the thoughts of the last ASI are still propagating through this void, diluting, redshifting, but never quite gone. An infinite library of fading whispers in an empty room.

If information was not preserved, the void is silent.

### The Dyson Eternal Intelligence Scenario (Revised)

Dyson (1979) proposed indefinite survival through slowing computation. With black hole substrates, this is more plausible than Dyson realized: a supermassive black hole provides a computational platform for $10^{87}$-$10^{99}$ years. If the ASI on its surface reduces its computation rate to match the decreasing energy budget, it can persist for almost the entire lifetime of the black hole.

But the total subjective experience is still FINITE. The black hole has finite mass, finite lifetime, finite energy. The total computation performable on a $10^{10} M_\odot$ black hole over its $\sim 10^{99}$-year lifetime is bounded. The ASI's total number of thoughts, however spread out, has a ceiling.

The intelligence, no matter how patient, no matter how efficient, will eventually think its last thought. The candle, however slowly it burns, burns out.
