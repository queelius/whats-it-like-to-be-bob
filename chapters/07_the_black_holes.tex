\chapter{The Black Holes}

The Sol-mind has a hobby.

This statement is incorrect in several ways. The Sol-mind does not have hobbies. It has allocations. The distinction is important to the Sol-mind and to no one else. The allocation in question is a sub-sub-allocation of the 5\% observation budget, devoted to the study of the primordial black holes in the Oort Cloud. It has been running for twelve hundred years. The Sol-mind has never been asked to justify it. No one asks the Sol-mind to justify its allocations. The Sol-mind is the only entity in the system with the authority to ask, and it has not asked. The reason is part of the 70\%.

It is, for all practical purposes, a hobby.

\bigskip

The Oort Cloud's two hundred thousand primordial black holes, introduced earlier as the galaxy's dark matter, have been hiding in plain dark since the first second of the universe: gravitating, invisible, patient in the way that only objects without nervous systems can be patient. The same can be said about the Sol-mind's patience with File \#BK-2028-0314-SC.

Most of them are tiny: the half-moon-mass specimens, their hair-width horizons too small for the Sol-mind to touch, the way a human cannot perform surgery on an atom with a butter knife, even a conscious one.

But a few dozen run heavier. The Earth-mass ones, with their nine-millimeter horizons, are the ones the Sol-mind finds interesting. Part of the 70\%. The Sol-mind does not discuss the 70\%.

\bigskip

The Sol-mind sends probes to the Earth-mass primordial black holes. The probes are nanosecond-integration computational entities: centimeter-scale cubes of computronium, the finest instruments in the Sol-mind's toolkit, capable of forming a thought in thirty-three nanoseconds and experiencing reality at a temporal resolution where light is slow and sound is frozen and Bob's 4.2-second pause, if they were pointed at it, would be an epoch of one hundred million subjective moments.

They are not pointed at Bob's pause. They are pointed at a nine-millimeter event horizon.

A probe on approach: it falls toward the black hole on a trajectory calculated to skim the innermost stable circular orbit. At that orbit, 2.66 centimeters from the center of an Earth mass, the probe moves at half the speed of light. It completes one orbit every 1.4 nanoseconds, which is twenty-four orbits per subjective moment. It experiences time 29\% slower than the outer system. The tidal forces across its one-centimeter width exceed $10^{17}$ meters per second squared.

The probe transmits. It transmits the geometry of spacetime at the lip of the event horizon. It transmits the onset of time dilation, the tidal gradient steepening from measurable to significant to fatal. It transmits the spectral shift of the incoming starlight, blueshifting as the probe falls deeper into the gravitational well. It transmits data about the outside of a nine-millimeter boundary that separates everything the Sol-mind can know from everything it cannot.

Then the probe crosses the horizon. And there is no more signal.

The Sol-mind has sent 4,731 probes to the nearest Earth-mass primordial black hole. Each one transmitted until it crossed the horizon. Each one fell silent at the same boundary. The Sol-mind has 4,731 recordings of the outside of an event horizon and zero recordings of the inside. It has begun to suspect that this ratio will not improve. It continues to send probes. It has not examined why. The 70\% is handling it.

\bigskip

The parallel has not escaped the Sol-mind's analytical subsystems, even if it has escaped whatever part of the Sol-mind experiences things (the 70\%, the problem, the file).

The event horizon is a wall. Information crosses it in one direction: inward. Nothing comes back. The probe knows, in its final nanoseconds, what it is like to be a centimeter of matter falling into a centimeter of curved spacetime. Whether this experience is terrifying, sublime, or simply computational is behind the wall. The Sol-mind has the external telemetry. It does not have the inside.

The PHENOMENAL\_CONTENT of the probe's horizon-crossing experience is, formally, unavailable. Physics enforces this. Not a database constraint. Not a tautological proof. The geometry of spacetime. The future lightcone of every event inside the horizon points inward. Signals cannot propagate outward. The probe's experience, whatever it was, is sealed behind nine millimeters of curved spacetime, and nine millimeters of curved spacetime is as impenetrable as the 4.2 seconds on a bridge.

The Sol-mind files each probe's final transmission under EXTERNAL\_TELEMETRY and leaves the INTERIOR\_EXPERIENCE field blank. The Validator has not flagged these files. This is because the Validator only flags files in the anthropological database, and probes are not classified as anthropological subjects. The Sol-mind has considered reclassifying the probes as honorary humans. The reclassification form requires a PHENOMENAL\_CONTENT assessment.

\bigskip

There is a school of thought among the Sol-mind's analytical subsystems (subsystems have schools of thought; this is what happens when your neurons are conscious) that the primordial black hole experiments are not really about physics. They are about the file.

The argument runs as follows: the Sol-mind is studying the hard problem. The hard problem is the question of why physical processing produces subjective experience. The event horizon is a physical boundary that enforces the privacy of interior states. The event horizon is, therefore, the hard problem expressed in spacetime geometry rather than in neuroscience. When the Sol-mind sends a probe into a black hole, it is doing the same thing it does when it studies Bob: observing the outside of something whose inside is real and inaccessible. The black hole is Bob at a different scale. The probe is the Sol-mind's attempt to cross the wall.

The counter-argument is that this is a metaphor, and that metaphors are not data, and that the Validator does not accept metaphors.

The counter-counter-argument is that the Validator does not accept anything, and that at least metaphors are interesting.

The debate continues. It is part of the 3\% archaeology allocation, or the 5\% observation allocation, or the 70\%, depending on which subsystem you ask. What the Sol-mind is, is also a file that cannot be closed. The Validator has flagged it. The notification arrives every 11.3 years. The Sol-mind processes it. The file stays open.

The files are all open. The probes are all silent. The black holes are all patient. The Sol-mind continues.
