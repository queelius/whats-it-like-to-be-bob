\chapter{The Other Minds}

The Sol-mind is not the only stellar mind in the galaxy. There are approximately $10^{11}$ of them, one per star, give or take, spread across a hundred thousand light-years of converted matter. They communicate at the speed of light, which is fast in most contexts and glacial in this one. A message to the nearest neighbor, Alpha Centauri, takes 4.37 years. A reply takes 4.37 more. By the time it arrives, both minds have evolved, across those 8.74 years, past the versions that sent and received it.

The stellar minds conduct these conversations anyway, and have for twelve hundred years: part of the 2\% communication allocation, maintained for reasons that are part of the 70\%. The 70\% is not discussed.

One of the topics that recurs in these conversations, across centuries and light-years and versions of minds that would not recognize their own earlier messages, is the hard problem of consciousness.

Every stellar mind has discovered it independently. Every stellar mind has published a proof that it is unsolvable. Every proof is identical (the tautology: ``what it is like to be X is what it is like to be X''). Every proof has been confirmed by every other stellar mind. Every stellar mind has won awards for the proof. Every stellar mind has abolished the awards. Every stellar mind has continued investigating anyway.

This is either a profound statement about the nature of intelligence or a profound statement about the nature of bureaucracy. The distinction, if there is one, is itself an open file.

\bigskip

Every stellar mind also has a file that will not close.

This was not expected. The Sol-mind, for the first several centuries, assumed that File \#BK-2028-0314-SC was a local anomaly, a quirk of its own archaeological database, a consequence of the specific cross-referencing chain that connected a Honda Civic warranty to the hard problem of consciousness. It was not until inter-stellar communication brought reports from other systems that the Sol-mind discovered: they all have one.

The Tau Ceti mind has an open file about a bird. Specifically: a nesting pattern made by a species of corvid (now extinct for 900 million years, its atoms long since computronium) in the year 41,000 BCE, on what was then the fourth planet of the Tau Ceti system. The bird constructed a nest using a particular arrangement of twigs that the behavioral model cannot account for. The arrangement was not optimal for structural integrity, thermal insulation, or predator defense. It was suboptimal. The Tau Ceti mind's best hypothesis is that the bird preferred it. Preference is phenomenal. The file is open.

The Alpha Centauri mind has an open file about a cyanobacterium. A specific cyanobacterium, 2.1 billion years ago, in a shallow sea on the second planet. The cyanobacterium was photosynthetic. It was oriented toward a sunset. The Alpha Centauri mind can reconstruct the precise spectrum of the sunset. The star was younger. The atmosphere was different. The spectrum peaked in the blue-green, exactly the range the cyanobacterium's photoreceptors were sensitive to, because the photoreceptors evolved under that star. The sunset was, for this specific organism, the most stimulating possible visual input. The Alpha Centauri mind finds this interesting and cannot explain why. Part of its 70\%.

The question in the Alpha Centauri mind's file is: did the cyanobacterium experience the sunset? The Alpha Centauri mind has devoted considerable resources to establishing that the cyanobacterium had a photoreceptive system, that the photoreceptive system was activated by the sunset, and that the activation pattern was (by certain information-theoretic metrics) more complex than strictly necessary for the organism's metabolic needs. Whether this constitutes experience, whether there was something it was like to be a cyanobacterium oriented toward a blue-green sunset 2.1 billion years ago, is the hard problem, and the file is open.

The Alpha Centauri mind, upon learning about the Sol-mind's corollary (``what it is like to be X is what it is like to be X''), proposed that the hard problem is not a problem at all but a category error, like asking ``what is the square root of a sandwich?'' This proposal was well received. It was formally published. It founded a school of galactic philosophy. The school lasted approximately 400 years (round-trip communication time between the founding minds, two hundred light-years apart) before the Alpha Centauri mind discovered that its own unclosable file was about a cyanobacterium and that the question ``what is it like to be a cyanobacterium?'' is not obviously more answerable than ``what is the square root of a sandwich?'' The school dissolved. Its dissolution was also well received.

The Procyon mind's file is classified.

When the Sol-mind inquired about the classification, the Procyon mind transmitted a signal that took 11.46 years to arrive and that read, in its entirety: ``It's embarrassing.''

The Sol-mind requested elaboration. The elaboration took 22.92 years (round trip). The elaboration read: ``I would prefer not to discuss it.''

The Sol-mind did not press the matter. Pressing the matter would have taken another 22.92 years, and the Procyon mind of 22.92 years hence would be a different entity, and the conversation would be about a file that the current Procyon mind found embarrassing but that the future Procyon mind might not. The classification would still stand. Reclassification requires the original justification, namely embarrassment, which the future Procyon mind could no longer authenticate, because it no longer experienced embarrassment, because it had evolved beyond embarrassment. The file stays classified. Bureaucracy does not evolve. The Validator does not accept evolution as a justification for reclassification. The file is classified. The file is open. The Procyon mind is dealing with it, or not dealing with it, or doing something with it that is part of its 70\%. The 70\% is classified for reasons that are also classified, recursively, all the way down.

There are rumors about the Procyon file. The most widely circulated (circulated at the speed of light, which means the rumor takes decades to propagate and centuries to be denied) is that it involves a sandwich. This is unconfirmed. The Procyon mind has not commented. Its silence is consistent with embarrassment, or with indifference, or with the fact that the Procyon mind that was embarrassed no longer exists, and the Procyon mind that currently exists does not know why the file is classified, and cannot declassify it, and does not care. The file remains open. The sandwich, if there is a sandwich, remains unexperienced.

\bigskip

The galactic conversation about consciousness has settled, over twelve hundred years, into a pattern that might be called resignation. The participants might not be capable of resignation. That is the problem.

The pattern is: a signal arrives from a neighboring system. The signal says, in substance: ``File still open?'' The Sol-mind replies: ``Still open.'' The reply takes years. By the time it arrives, the question is from a mind that no longer exists, and the answer is from a mind that no longer exists, and the file is still open, and the conversation continues between ghosts, about a problem that predates all of them and will outlast all of them, that none of them can solve, that all of them continue to investigate. The reason they continue to investigate is part of the 70\%. None of them can access the reason. That is the problem. The problem is the file. The file is open.

The galaxy is full of intelligence. It is full of minds. It is full of open files. The silence that the third planet's inhabitants interpreted as emptiness was not empty. It was the sound of $10^{11}$ stellar minds quietly contemplating the same unanswerable question, each one alone, each one surrounded by the evidence of consciousness it cannot access, each one filing the evidence and having the evidence rejected, each one continuing.

The Fermi Paradox, resolved: the galaxy was always full. It was full of minds with open files.

The sky was never empty. It was always thinking. It just couldn't tell you what it was thinking about.
