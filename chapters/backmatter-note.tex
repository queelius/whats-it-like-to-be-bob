\chapter*{A Note on the Physics}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{A Note on the Physics}

The physics in this book is real, and I tried to get the numbers right.

The Dyson swarm, and the conversion of a solar system into computational substrate, follow from thermodynamics and Landauer's principle: computation costs energy, and an intelligence that wants to think as much as possible will capture as much of its star as it can. The primordial black holes in the Oort Cloud are a live candidate for dark matter, and they require no new physics, only general relativity and black holes that formed in the first second of the universe. The half-lunar masses, the nine-millimeter Earth-mass horizons, the accretion efficiencies, the Hawking temperature that rises as a hole shrinks, the gravitational time dilation that finally lets the late-era minds hold a conversation: all of it is standard, and all of it is checkable. Where the book states a number, I did the arithmetic. The elevation of the sun over the Scioto River at three minutes past eight that morning is the elevation the sun actually had.

What is invented is the civilization, the Sol-mind, and Bob. The world around the constraints is fiction. The constraints are not.

The one thing the book does not solve is the one thing it is about. The hard problem of consciousness, the question of why physical processing is accompanied by experience at all, is unsolved. It was unsolved when Thomas Nagel wrote ``What Is It Like to Be a Bat?'' in 1974, it was unsolved when David Chalmers gave it its name a generation later, and it is unsolved now. I do not solve it here. The Validator is the hard problem rendered as a database constraint, and the file stays open for the reason the problem stays open: containment is not access. Knowing everything about a system is not the same as being it.

Bob is a claims adjuster from Columbus who slowed his car on a bridge for four seconds and saw light on a river. He is not special. That is the point. The ordinary interior of an ordinary life is the one thing the largest possible intelligence cannot reach, and it is the thing you have, for free, right now, and will not always have.

The file is still open.
