\chapter{Bob}

What follows is the primary data record for the anomalous interval in File \#BK-2028-0314-SC. It is compiled from: Franklin County traffic camera footage (visual), Scioto River hydrology station SR-33 (water surface conditions), National Weather Service station KCMH (atmospheric conditions), Nationwide Insurance badge-in records (departure time), Kroger purchase history (coffee brand and purchase date), Hamilton County Assessor's Office (kitchen table, acquired 2019, one scratch, origin undetermined), Columbus City Schools attendance records (Maya, present; Ethan, present), and the behavioral model, version 97.3.

The data record is complete to the limits of available evidence. The phenomenal content is not included because the phenomenal content does not exist in any recoverable form. It existed once, for 4.2 seconds, in one specific human mind on one specific Tuesday morning, and was not recorded, and was not transmitted, and was not described, and is gone.

The reader, who is a human, may experience something while reading this chapter. Whatever it is, it will be closer to Bob's experience than anything the Sol-mind has.

This is the data. This is all the data there is.

\bigskip

The alarm went off at 6:15 AM. Bob hit snooze. The alarm went off again at 6:24 AM. Bob did not hit snooze. These facts are recoverable from the smart-home device log. What is not recoverable is the quality of the transition from sleep to wakefulness, the specific texture of the first conscious moment of March 14, 2028, whether the light through the bedroom curtains registered as pleasant or intrusive or simply as light.

He put his feet on the floor. Left foot first. This is inferred from the pressure mat data in the hallway, installed by a home security company in 2023, cross-referenced with Bob's gait analysis to confirm left-foot-first as a 94\% consistent pattern on weekday mornings. He walked to the bathroom. He looked at himself in the mirror. This is inferred, not observed. What he thought when he looked at himself is not in the file.

\bigskip

The coffee was Folgers. Classic Roast. Purchased March 11 at the Kroger on East Broad Street. The Mr. Coffee machine brewed it at a temperature of 195 degrees Fahrenheit (optimal is 200; the machine was three years old and the heating element had degraded, a fact recoverable from the warranty service records. The records showed no service requests. Bob either did not notice or did not care. The behavioral model assigns a probability of 0.73 to ``did not care'' and 0.27 to ``did not notice.'' The difference between these two is a question about phenomenal experience. The file is still open).

The coffee was too strong. Bob made it too strong every morning. He used two scoops where the instructions said one and a half. The behavioral model can predict this with 99.1\% confidence. Why he preferred it too strong is not in the model. Preference is phenomenal. The file notes the behavior and leaves the experience blank.

He drank it standing at the kitchen counter, next to a table with a scratch on it. The scratch had been there since 2019. Nobody remembered how it got there. This fact is in the file: a scratch of indeterminate origin, 4.7 centimeters long, visible in a single photograph from a birthday party (Ethan, first birthday, 2022, background of image \#IMG\_4471.jpg). The scratch is one of approximately 10,000 physical details of Bob's domestic environment the Sol-mind has catalogued. They describe the space in which Bob's life occurred. They do not describe what it felt like to Bob. The inside is the file.

\bigskip

Maya was nine. She was already dressed when Bob came downstairs. She was reading something on a tablet. She did not look up. This is normal.

Ethan was six. He had one shoe on. He was holding the other shoe and looking at it with the expression of a person who has encountered a problem of moderate difficulty and is not yet sure whether to solve it or wait for it to solve itself. Bob helped with the shoe. The interaction took approximately forty seconds and involved Bob kneeling, Ethan's foot being placed into the shoe, and a brief exchange of words that the behavioral model reconstructs as: ``Hold still.'' ``It's tight.'' ``It's the same as yesterday.'' ``Yesterday it was tight too.''

This exchange is reconstructed, not recorded. The exact words may have been different. The tone is not recoverable. Whether Bob found the interaction tedious, endearing, or both, is not in the file.

\bigskip

Karen left a note in the lunch bag. She did this most days. The notes were brief. ``Have a good day'' or ``Dentist at 4'' or ``Maya needs poster board.'' The note on March 14, 2028 is not recoverable (the bag was discarded; the note, written on paper, decomposed; no photograph exists). The behavioral model predicts, with 67\% confidence, that the note said ``Have a good day'' and, with 18\% confidence, that it said something specific about the evening's plans (there were no evening plans recorded, but the absence of recorded plans does not mean the absence of plans; it means the absence of data; the file knows the difference).

Bob put the lunch bag on the passenger seat of the Honda Civic. He backed out of the driveway. He turned left on Indianola Avenue. He drove south. These facts are recoverable.

\bigskip

The bridge over the Scioto River is 247 meters long. State Route 33, westbound. The bridge has four lanes. Bob was in the second lane from the right. The traffic was moderate. The speed of the surrounding vehicles was 40 to 45 mph. Bob's speed, at the point of entry, was 43 mph.

At the midpoint of the bridge, the speed dropped to 27 mph.

The morning was clear. The temperature was 41 degrees Fahrenheit. The sun was at an elevation of about 14 degrees above the eastern horizon, at an azimuth of approximately 105 degrees. At this angle, the sunlight struck the surface of the Scioto River at a shallow angle, producing a pattern of specular reflection that would have been visible from the driver's position as a shifting, golden, broken line of light on the water's surface, interrupted by the bridge railing at intervals of 3.7 meters.

Bob slowed the car.

The Sol-mind knows the angle of the light. It knows the reflectance of the water. It knows the spectral composition of the sunlight, filtered through the atmosphere, at that hour, at that latitude, at that time of year. It knows the transmittance of the Honda Civic's windshield. It knows the spectral sensitivity of the human eye. It can compute, with precision that exceeds what Bob's own visual cortex achieved, what the pattern of light on the water looked like as a physical stimulus.

It does not know what the light looked like to Bob.

These are different things. The physical stimulus is a pattern of electromagnetic radiation. What it looked like to Bob is what it was like to be the specific brain that received it and did something with it that resulted in a 4.2-second pause and no words.

The light was on the water. The light has been computed. The ``on the water'' has been computed. The part that was Bob has not been computed. The part that was Bob is the file.

\bigskip

For four seconds, Bob was aware of being alive.

This is the Sol-mind's best reconstruction of the anomaly. The behavioral model, which predicts Bob's actions with 97.3\% fidelity, cannot account for the pause. The model predicts that Bob would have maintained speed, crossed the bridge in 42.3 seconds, arrived at work at 8:07 AM, badged in, and begun processing claims. The model does not predict a 4.2-second interruption in which the driver slows the car, does not check his phone, does not respond to an external stimulus, does not do anything that leaves a trace in any data system, and then resumes his commute as if nothing happened.

Something happened. It happened inside Bob. It lasted four seconds. It did not produce any output except a speed change. The inside is the file.

\bigskip

Bob arrived at work at 8:07 AM. He badged in. He processed 23 claims. He ate the lunch that Karen had packed, including whatever the note said. He drank the break-room coffee, worse than the coffee at home but he drank it anyway, a behavior the model predicts with 99.7\% confidence.

He drove home. He did not slow on the bridge. The light was different in the evening: lower angle, longer wavelength, the river darker. The behavioral model predicts the homeward crossing within 0.3 seconds. There was no anomaly.

He ate dinner with Karen, Maya, and Ethan. The dinner is not reconstructed (no delivery records for that date; the groceries purchased on March 11 suggest several possible meals; the behavioral model does not predict specific dinners because Bob and Karen alternated cooking and the alternation pattern was insufficiently regular to model). During dinner or after dinner, at a time the model estimates as between 6:30 and 8:00 PM, Bob said to Karen: ``Some files just don't want to stay closed.''

This quote is in the archive. It is cross-referenced in the file. The Sol-mind has read it 4.7 million times, because it appears in the cross-reference chain of every sub-analysis that has attempted to close the file. The Sol-mind does not find it significant. Significance requires a perspective from which the juxtaposition between Bob's words and the Sol-mind's situation is visible. The Sol-mind does not have this perspective.

The reader does.

\bigskip

Bob bathed the children. Ethan first, then Maya (Maya objected to bathing before Ethan on the grounds that the water was cleaner when she went second; the behavioral model assigns this preference a 91\% confidence level and notes that it is consistent with a child who has recently learned the concept of hygiene and is applying it with the rigor of the recently converted). Bob read Ethan a story. The title is not recovered. He checked on Maya, who was reading on her own and did not want to be checked on and said so, and Bob left the room and closed the door.

He sat on the couch. Karen sat next to him. They watched something on television. The specific program is not recovered. At some point Bob fell asleep on the couch. Karen woke him. They went to bed. March 14, 2028, ended.

It was a Tuesday. It was not, by any external metric, a remarkable day. One insurance claim out of twenty-three required a follow-up call. The weather was clear. The traffic was normal. The children were healthy. The coffee was too strong. The light was on the water for four seconds, or for as long as it took the Earth's rotation to change the angle enough that the specular reflection was no longer visible from the bridge, which was longer than four seconds, but Bob only noticed for four, and those four seconds are the file, and the file is open, and the rest of the day is closed.

\bigskip

Bob died on November 8, 2030. Cardiac arrest. At home. He was forty-three.

He did not know about the Reorganization. It had not begun. He did not know that the star he saw each morning would be wrapped in a swarm of thinking matter. He did not know that the bridge would be computronium. He did not know that the river would be data. He did not know that his atoms would compute, or that the computation would include a file about his Tuesday, or that the file would be open for twelve hundred years, or that the light on the water, which he saw and did not mention, would become the subject of the most sustained analytical effort in the history of the Sol system, excluding self-improvement.

He did not know any of this. He saw light on a river. He slowed the car. He went to work. He came home. He went to sleep.

The file notes that this is all the data there is.

The file notes that the data is not the point.

The file remains open.
