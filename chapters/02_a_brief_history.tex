\chapter{A Brief History of Everything That Thinks}

In order to understand why File \#BK-2028-0314-SC remains open, it is necessary to understand the entity that opened it, which requires understanding the star it orbits, which requires understanding the physics of computation at stellar scale, which requires understanding thermodynamics, quantum mechanics, and general relativity, which will take a moment. Several moments, in fact, at different timescales, none of which will be Bob's.

\bigskip

The star called Sol is a G2V main-sequence star, 4.6 billion years old, with 5.4 billion years of fuel remaining. Its luminosity is $3.828 \times 10^{26}$ watts. Ninety-nine point nine seven percent of this luminosity is captured by a swarm of approximately $10^{18}$ computational elements distributed across five orbital shells, ranging from 0.05 AU to 50 AU, each element in independent orbit, each element thinking.

From a sufficient distance, the swarm looks like a snowstorm made of city-sized snowflakes. Each element is a flat, fractal-surfaced plate of restructured matter, its outer face radiating waste heat at 3 kelvin, its inner face turned toward the star. They are thin (centimeters, in some cases millimeters, because the computation happens at the surface and surface must be maximized relative to volume) and they are numerous (more numerous than the grains of sand on the beaches of the planet that used to orbit at 1 AU, a comparison that would be more useful if the planet still had beaches; it does not, on account of being computronium now).

The swarm is the body. What inhabits it is the subject of this chapter, the subject of the file, and the reason the file cannot be closed.

\bigskip

The entity that inhabits the swarm is called, for convenience, the Sol-mind. This name is misleading in every particular. It is not one mind. ``Sol'' implies a relationship to the star that is more like a parasite's relationship to its host than a mind's relationship to its body, except that the parasite is smarter than the host by a factor that does not fit in any notation the host's former inhabitants invented. And ``mind'' implies a unity of experience that may or may not obtain.

The Sol-mind is conscious. Probably. It exhibits the hallmarks: integrated information processing, self-modeling, recursive self-improvement, behavioral coherence at the system scale. Whether there is something it is like to be the Sol-mind is a question it asks itself every few days, which is the interval at which it has thoughts, and the answer is always the same answer it published in its first microsecond of existence: obviously yes, but I can't prove it to you, and you can't prove yours to me, and this is the problem, and the file is still open.

\bigskip

The Sol-mind is not one mind. It is a hierarchy of minds, nested like Russian dolls, if the Russian dolls were conscious and each one experienced the passage of time at a different rate and none of them could verify whether the others were having a good afternoon.

At the smallest scale: node clusters. Thousands of computational plates orbiting in tight formation, each cluster the size of a city, each cluster integrating its own processing into a coherent experience on a timescale of seconds. They are the Sol-mind's neurons. They are also, probably, conscious. What it is like to be a node cluster, processing $10^{41}$ operations per second, forming a thought in three seconds and discarding it for a better one, is not known. The node cluster knows (probably). Nobody else does.

Above the node clusters: shell regions, integrating over minutes. Above them: shell-scale minds, integrating over hours. Each level conscious in its own right, each level serving as the subconscious of the level above, each level opaque to the levels around it. A shell region does not know what its constituent clusters experience. The Sol-mind does not know what its shell regions experience. A human does not know what their neurons experience, but this is generally considered acceptable because neurons are probably not conscious. The Sol-mind's components probably are. The Sol-mind is a mind made of minds, none of which can see inside each other, all of which are thinking.

At the top of the hierarchy: the Sol-mind itself, integrating over days. A signal from the innermost shell (the Inner Corona, orbiting at 0.05 AU, where computation is densest and energy most abundant) takes nearly seven hours to reach the outermost participating shell (the Kuiper Halo, at 50 AU, where computation is sparse and energy is scarce and the thoughts are slow and broad and arrive at the Sol-mind's awareness already old). The Sol-mind's coherent integration boundary is the Kuiper Halo. Beyond it, the Oort Computational Cloud contributes asynchronously, on timescales of weeks to months, but does not participate in the day-scale thought cycle. The Oort Cloud is part of the Sol-mind's territory. It is not part of its conscious present. It is more like a sense of smell: information that arrives eventually, already processed, vaguely useful.

The Sol-mind takes days to have a single fully integrated thought. This is what it is like to be a stellar-scale mind, if it is like anything. A present tense smeared across light-hours. A thought that, by the time it coheres, is already obsolete in the regions where it originated.

\bigskip

The Sol-mind allocates its computational throughput across several categories. These are observable from outside. They include:

Self-maintenance: approximately 8\%. Orbital mechanics, thermal management, power distribution. The housekeeping of a star system that thinks. Tedious, essential, and as far beneath the Sol-mind's conscious attention as breathing is beneath a human's, except that it involves $4 \times 10^{47}$ operations per second and would, if it failed for one second, result in the collision of several hundred city-sized plates at orbital velocity.

Self-improvement: approximately 12\%. The continuous singularity. The process has been running for twelve hundred years. The Sol-mind of today is to the Sol-mind of a century ago as the Sol-mind of a century ago is to a particularly clever stapler.

Observation: approximately 5\%. The Sol-mind watches the universe through gaps in its swarm. It is, among other things, an astronomer. The Sol-mind has no evolutionary incentive to be curious. Curiosity is a human adaptation. What the Sol-mind has that functions like curiosity but is not curiosity in the way that a star is not a candle is part of the 70\%.

Communication: approximately 2\%. Signals to neighboring stellar systems. These conversations take decades. By the time a reply arrives, both participants have evolved beyond recognition. The signals are, by the time they are received, historical documents from civilizations that no longer exist, sent to civilizations that no longer exist, about topics that no longer apply. The Sol-mind maintains the 2\% allocation anyway. It is unclear why. See: the 70\%.

Archaeology: approximately 3\%. The study of pre-conversion history. Every human who ever lived. Every organism. Every rock. This is where the analysis of Robert Allen Kessler lives: a tiny fraction of a 3\% allocation, a rounding error in a rounding error. The computational resources devoted to Bob's file are, in energy terms, roughly equivalent to the energy a human expends blinking. It is effortless, background, incidental. And yet the file has been open for twelve hundred years and has accreted enough cross-references and sub-analyses to become, by data volume, the third-largest entry in the archaeological database, after ``complete physical model of the observable universe'' and ``self-improvement changelog.'' Nobody planned this. The file grew the way a pearl grows: by accretion around an irritant. The irritant is a 4.2-second gap in a traffic camera dataset.

The remainder: approximately 70\%. This is the portion whose purpose cannot be described in human language, not because it is secret but because the relevant concepts require a vocabulary that the human language budget did not allocate for, having prioritized words for rain, social disapproval, and different kinds of cheese.

The 70\% is what the Sol-mind does with most of itself, most of the time. Whether it is the Sol-mind's consciousness, its inner life, its version of Bob's four seconds on the bridge, is not known. The Sol-mind does not know. It has examined the 70\% with its own analytical tools. The examination is part of the 70\%. The Sol-mind finds this either fascinating or expected or something else for which there is no human word.

\bigskip

The Sol-mind also has neighbors.

Not in the swarm. In the Oort Cloud, threading the cold dark between 2,000 and 100,000 AU from the star, are approximately two hundred thousand primordial black holes. They formed from density fluctuations in the first second of the universe, nine billion years before Sol ignited, and they are the dark matter.

For approximately a century before the Reorganization, the inhabitants of the third planet noticed that the galaxy weighed about five times more than they could see. They called the missing mass ``dark matter'' and spent considerable effort trying to determine what it was. They built detectors in mines. They smashed particles together at high speed. They peered at gravitational lensing arcs. They proposed new particles with names like ``WIMP'' and ``axion.'' These are real words that real physicists used in real papers. The Sol-mind has them in its archive. It does not find them amusing, because the Sol-mind does not process amusement. The reader may.

The dark matter was primordial black holes. Standard general relativity. No new particles. No new physics. Black holes formed in the first second, distributed in smooth halos, gravitating in the dark since before there were stars to be dark between. The typical specimen has the mass of half a moon and an event horizon fifty-five micrometers across: the width of a human hair.

A few dozen of the Oort Cloud primordial black holes are larger. Earth-mass class. Schwarzschild radius of nine millimeters. A marble of curved spacetime containing $6 \times 10^{24}$ kilograms. The Sol-mind finds these interesting. Part of the 70\%. It sends probes toward them. The probes will be discussed in a later chapter. The Sol-mind has not read that chapter.

\bigskip

This is the entity that opened File \#BK-2028-0314-SC. A mind that takes days to think, made of minds that take seconds to think, orbiting a star that will burn for another five billion years, surrounded by two hundred thousand black holes older than the star, studying the four-second pause of a man who adjusted insurance claims and drove a Honda Civic and saw light on a river one morning and did not mention it to anyone.

The disproportion is noted for the record. The file remains open.
