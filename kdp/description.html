A post-singularity galactic civilization has converted the galaxy's matter to computation. It studies one man's Tuesday.

Robert Allen Kessler, claims adjuster, Columbus, Ohio, saw light on a river on March 14, 2028 and felt four seconds of happiness he never told anyone about. Twelve hundred years later, a stellar-scale intelligence devotes a fraction of its vast resources to reconstructing every recoverable detail of that day. It knows his shoe size. It knows the brand of his coffee. It knows the scratch on his kitchen table. It cannot know what the light looked like to him.

Nine chapters zoom from cosmological scale to one man's morning and back out to the thermodynamic death of the universe. The physics is real. The numbers are right. The hard problem of consciousness is the last problem, and it outlasts the stars.

Hard science fiction for readers of Ted Chiang, Greg Egan, and Peter Watts.

A novella (~21,000 words)
